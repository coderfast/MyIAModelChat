The reproduction of flowering plants is a complex process that involves pollination, fertilization, and the formation of seeds and fruits. Flowers contain male reproductive organs (stamens that produce pollen) and female organs (pistil that contains ovules). Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the stamens to the stigma of the pistil, which can occur by wind, water, or animal pollinators. Pollinators, including bees, butterflies, hummingbirds, and bats, are essential for the reproduction of many species of flowering plants. After pollination, the pollen germinates and the pollen tube grows toward the ovule, where fertilization occurs. The fertilized ovule develops into a seed, and the ovary of the flower develops into a fruit.

Photosynthesis is the fundamental biological process that sustains virtually all life on Earth. Through this process, plants, algae, and some bacteria convert the energy of sunlight into chemical energy stored in sugar bonds, using carbon dioxide (CO2) from the air and water from the soil. The simplified equation for photosynthesis is: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2. This process not only produces the food that plants need to grow, but also produces the oxygen we breathe. Photosynthesis is responsible for the chemical composition of the Earth's atmosphere and has been the engine that has allowed the evolution of complex life on our planet.

Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts, specialized organelles found in plant cells. Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, a green pigment that absorbs sunlight, primarily in the red and blue regions of the visible spectrum. The light absorbed by chlorophyll is used to excite electrons, which are then used to produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate) and NADPH (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate), two high-energy molecules used in the dark phase of photosynthesis. The dark phase, also known as the Calvin cycle, uses ATP and NADPH to fix CO2 into sugar molecules. This process occurs in all green parts of plants, but is particularly efficient in leaves, which have a large surface area to capture sunlight.

The rate of photosynthesis is determined by several environmental factors that can limit the process. Pollination can occur in two main ways: self-pollination, where pollen is transferred within the same flower or between flowers of the same plant, and cross-pollination, where pollen is transferred between flowers of different plants of the same species. Pollination can be carried out by wind, water, or animals, each with specific flower adaptations to facilitate the process.
