## Chapter 2: Spain's Accession to the European Communities

### 2.1 The Application and Negotiation Process

Spain's accession to the European Communities was long and complex, lasting more than a decade from the initial application in 1977 to effective entry in 1986. The formal application for accession was submitted by the government of Adolfo Suárez in July 1977, a few months after the first democratic elections following the Franco dictatorship. The application was motivated by the desire to consolidate the democratic transition, promote economic development, and integrate Spain into the mainstream of Europe. The European Commission issued a positive opinion on Spain's application in 1979, but accession negotiations were delayed due to multiple factors, including the need to adapt Spanish legislation to the acquis communautaire, concerns of other Member States about the impact of Spain's accession on the Common Agricultural Policy, and the need to reform European institutions to accommodate a new member. Accession negotiations officially began in February 1980, and focused on areas such as the free movement of goods, the Common Agricultural Policy, competition policy, transport, energy, research, education, and social policy. Negotiations were particularly difficult in areas such as agriculture, as Spain had a significant agricultural sector that required specific adaptations to integrate into the Common Agricultural Policy, and fisheries, as Spain had an important fishing fleet that required specific agreements with other Member States. Negotiations were completed in March 1985, and the Accession Treaty was signed in June 1985 in Madrid, in a ceremony attended by the Heads of State and Government of the Member States and Spain. The Accession Treaty was ratified by the parliaments of all Member States and by the Spanish Cortes, and Spain officially joined the European Communities on January 1, 1986.

### 2.2 The Conditions of Accession

Spain's accession to the European Communities was subject to multiple conditions that reflected both the accession criteria established by the Community and the specific needs of Spain and the existing Member States. The conditions included the adoption and implementation of the acquis communautaire in its entirety, which required the implementation of multiple legislative, administrative, and economic reforms in Spain to adapt its legislation and practices to European standards. The adaptation of the acquis communautaire included trade liberalization, adaptation of competition rules, implementation of consumer protection standards, adaptation of environmental standards, and the creation of institutions for the implementation of European policies. Spain's accession included transition periods in specific areas, such as agriculture, fisheries, and transport, which allowed Spain to gradually adapt to common policies without generating significant disruptions to its economy. The transition periods included the gradual adaptation of Spanish agricultural prices to common market prices, the adaptation of the Spanish fishing fleet to Community fishing rules, and the adaptation of the transport sector to European competition standards. Spain's accession also included specific provisions on the integration of Spanish regions, particularly the outermost regions such as the Canary Islands, Ceuta, and Melilla, which require specific treatment due to their geographical remoteness and their particular economic and social conditions. The European Commission established provisions for the outermost regions that include specific financial aid, tax exemptions, and rules adapted to their specific needs. Spain's accession also included provisions on fisheries, which was a fundamental economic sector for Spain, and which required specific agreements with other Member States to protect Spanish fishing interests.

## Appendix A: Chronology of Spain in the European Union

### 1977 – Application for Accession

In July 1977, the government of Adolfo Suárez submitted the formal application for Spain's accession to the European Communities, a few months after the first democratic elections following the Franco dictatorship. The application was motivated by the desire to consolidate the democratic transition, promote economic development, and integrate Spain into the mainstream of Europe. The European Commission issued a positive opinion on the application in 1979, but accession negotiations were delayed due to multiple factors. Accession negotiations officially began in February 1980, and focused on areas such as the free movement of goods, the Common Agricultural Policy, competition policy, transport, energy, research, education, and social policy. Negotiations were completed in March 1985, and the Accession Treaty was signed in June 1985 in Madrid, in a ceremony attended by the Heads of State and Government of the Member States and Spain. The Accession Treaty was ratified by the parliaments of all Member States and by the Spanish Cortes, and Spain officially joined the European Communities on January 1, 1986.

### 1986 – Accession to the European Communities

Spain officially joined the European Communities on January 1, 1986, becoming the fifth member state of the European Community. Spain's accession marked the beginning of a new era of economic, political, and cultural integration for Spain, and generated multiple benefits, including access to a large market, the attraction of foreign investment, job creation, improved competitiveness, and participation in European policy. Spain's accession also generated challenges, including the need to adapt to European standards, competition from other European producers, and the reduction of tariff protection. The European Commission positively assessed Spain's accession, and recognized that Spain had fulfilled its accession commitments and had implemented the necessary reforms to integrate into the European Communities.

### 1992 – Barcelona Olympic Games

The 1992 Barcelona Olympic Games were a historic event for Spain, as it was the first time that Spain hosted the Olympic Games, and the event generated significant economic, social, and cultural benefits for Barcelona and for Spain. The Barcelona Olympic Games promoted Spain's image in the world, and demonstrated Spain's capacity to organize high-level international events. The Barcelona Olympic Games also generated significant debates about the impact of international sporting events on host cities and countries, as some argue that the Olympic Games generate significant economic, social, and cultural benefits, while others argue that the Olympic Games generate significant costs and can cause social and environmental disruption. The European Commission recognized the importance of the Barcelona Olympic Games, and promoted the participation of other Member States in the Olympic Games and in other international sporting events.

### 1999 – Adoption of the Euro

Spain adopted the euro as its official currency on January 1, 1999, replacing the peseta, which had been the Spanish currency since 1869. Euro adoption was the culmination of an economic convergence process that included macroeconomic stabilization, inflation reduction, public deficit reduction, and exchange rate stabilization. Euro adoption generated significant benefits for Spain, including the elimination of exchange rate risk, the reduction of interest rates, greater price transparency, and greater integration into European financial markets. The European Commission positively assessed Spain's euro adoption, and recognized that the euro had provided monetary stability and promoted economic integration.

### 2008 – Economic Crisis

The 2008 economic crisis had a profound impact on Spain, as the housing bubble, high public deficit, growing public debt, and weakness of the financial sector generated a severe economic crisis that profoundly affected the Spanish economy and society. The economic crisis included a deep recession, with a 9 percent GDP decline between 2008 and 2013; dramatic unemployment, which reached 27 percent of the active population in 2013; a real estate crisis, with falling housing prices and the bankruptcy of multiple real estate developers; and a financial crisis, with the bankruptcy of multiple savings banks and the need for a financial bailout of the financial sector. The European Commission and the Spanish government implemented multiple measures to address the economic crisis, including structural reforms, fiscal consolidation, and financial support for the financial sector. The economic crisis generated significant debates about eurozone economic governance, the need to create financial support mechanisms, and the need to reform fiscal rules.

### 2020 – COVID-19 Pandemic

The COVID-19 pandemic significantly disrupted the Spanish economy and society, as the restriction of economic activities, the confinement of the population, and the reduction of tourism generated a severe economic and social crisis. Spain was one of the countries most affected by the COVID-19 pandemic, with one of the highest mortality rates in Europe, which highlighted the weaknesses of the Spanish health system and the need to improve European health cooperation. The European Commission and the Spanish government implemented multiple measures to address the pandemic, including the joint procurement of vaccines, financial support for health systems, the coordination of public health measures, and economic recovery funds. The COVID-19 pandemic also generated significant debates about the need to reform EU economic governance, the need to create financial support mechanisms, and the need to improve health cooperation between Member States. ## Appendix B: Glossary of European Terms

### Acquis Communautaire

The acquis communautaire is the body of rights and obligations that are common to all Member States of the European Union, and includes the rules, policies, and commitments established in the European treaties, European legislation, the case law of the Court of Justice, and common policies. The acquis communautaire is binding on all Member States, and must be implemented by Member States in their national legislation. The acquis communautaire includes multiple areas, including the free movement of goods, the free movement of persons, the free movement of services, the free movement of capital, the Common Agricultural Policy, competition policy, trade policy, monetary policy, cohesion policy, foreign and security policy, and cooperation in justice and home affairs.

### Copenhagen Criteria

The Copenhagen criteria are the criteria established by the Copenhagen European Council in 1993 that candidate countries must meet for accession to the European Union. The criteria include political criteria, such as the stability of institutions guaranteeing democracy, the rule of law, human rights, and the protection of minorities; economic criteria, such as the existence of a functioning market economy and the capacity to cope with competitive pressure and market forces within the Union; and accession criteria, such as the country's capacity to assume the obligations arising from membership, including the capacity to implement the acquis communautaire. The Copenhagen criteria are the criteria that candidate countries must meet to join the European Union, and are assessed by the European Commission and the European Council before accession.

### European Financial Stability Facility

The European Financial Stability Facility (EFSF) is a financial support fund created by the eurozone Member States in 2012 to provide financial assistance to Member States experiencing severe financial difficulties. The EFSF was created in response to the euro crisis, and has provided financial assistance to multiple Member States, including Greece, Ireland, Portugal, Spain, and Cyprus. The EFSF is financed by the eurozone Member States, and provides financial assistance in the form of conditional loans, which include the implementation of structural reforms, fiscal consolidation, and the improvement of competitiveness. The EFSF has been an important instrument for managing the euro crisis, and has contributed to the stabilization of the eurozone and the prevention of future financial crises.

### Bologna Process

The Bologna Process is a process for harmonizing the European higher education area that was launched in 1999 by 29 European countries, including Spain, with the objective of creating a European higher education area that promotes the mobility of students and teachers, the comparability of qualifications, and the employability of graduates. The Bologna Process establishes a common framework for university qualifications, which includes first-cycle (Bachelor's), second-cycle (Master's), and third-cycle (Doctoral) qualifications, and facilitates student mobility and the recognition of qualifications. The Bologna Process also promotes the quality of higher education, student participation, and the employability of graduates. Spain has actively participated in the Bologna Process, and has implemented the necessary reforms to adapt its higher education system to the European framework.


## Appendix C: Main Agreements and Treaties of Spain with the European Union

### 1985 Accession Treaty

The 1985 Accession Treaty was the treaty that established the conditions for Spain's accession to the European Communities, and was signed in Madrid in June 1985. The treaty established the conditions of accession, including the implementation of the acquis communautaire, transition periods in specific areas, provisions on fisheries, agriculture, and transport, and provisions on the outermost regions. The Accession Treaty was ratified by the parliaments of all Member States and by the Spanish Cortes, and Spain officially joined the European Communities on January 1, 1986. The Accession Treaty was a historic document that marked the beginning of Spain's integration into the European Communities, and established the bases for Spain's participation in European policy.

### 1992 Maastricht Treaty

The Maastricht Treaty, officially the Treaty on European Union, was signed in Maastricht in February 1992, and entered into force in November 1993. The treaty established the European Union, which replaced the European Communities, and expanded the Union's competences in areas such as foreign and security policy, cooperation in justice and home affairs, and European citizenship. The treaty also established the convergence criteria for adoption of the euro, which included inflation, public deficit, public debt, interest rates, and exchange rate stability criteria. Spain ratified the Maastricht Treaty in a referendum held in June 1992, in which 76 percent of voters voted in favor of ratification. The Maastricht Treaty was a historic document that transformed the European Communities into the European Union, and established the bases for the creation of the euro and the enlargement of the Union to Central and Eastern Europe.

### 1997 Amsterdam Treaty

The Amsterdam Treaty was signed in Amsterdam in October 1997, and entered into force in May 1999. The treaty amended the Treaty on European Union and the constituent treaties of the European Communities, and incorporated the Schengen Agreement, which establishes the free movement of persons within the European Union, into the Union's legal framework. The treaty also introduced new provisions on the protection of fundamental rights, cooperation in justice and home affairs, and foreign and security policy. Spain ratified the Amsterdam Treaty in a referendum held in March 1998, in which 67 percent of voters voted in favor of ratification. The Amsterdam Treaty was a significant document that strengthened fundamental rights, cooperation in justice and home affairs, and the free movement of persons within the European Union.

### 2001 Nice Treaty

The Nice Treaty was signed in Nice in February 2001, and entered into force in February 2003. The treaty amended the Treaty on European Union and the constituent treaties of the European Communities, and introduced significant changes to the structure of European institutions, including the reform of the European Commission, the reform of the European Council, and the reform of the European Parliament. The treaty also established the principle of enhanced cooperation, which allows a group of Member States to deepen cooperation in specific areas without the participation of all Member States. Spain ratified the Nice Treaty in a referendum held in February 2001, in which 77 percent of voters voted in favor of ratification. The Nice Treaty was a significant document that reformed European institutions and prepared the ground for the enlargement of the Union to Central and Eastern Europe.

### 2007 Lisbon Treaty

The Lisbon Treaty was signed in Lisbon in December 2007, and entered into force in December 2009. The treaty amended the Treaty on European Union and the constituent treaties of the European Communities, and introduced significant changes to the structure of European institutions, including the creation of the President of the European Council, the creation of the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, the expansion of the scope of the European Parliament, and the simplification of decision-making procedures. Spain ratified the Lisbon Treaty in a referendum held in February 2008, in which 76 percent of voters voted in favor of ratification. The Lisbon Treaty was a historic document that reformed European institutions and strengthened the capacity of the European Union to address the challenges of the 21st century.

### 2020 United Kingdom Withdrawal Agreement

The Agreement on the Withdrawal of the United Kingdom from the European Union, commonly known as Brexit, was signed in January 2020, and entered into force in February 2020. The agreement established the conditions for the withdrawal of the United Kingdom from the European Union, including citizens' rights, financial obligations, the management of the Irish border, and transition arrangements.
