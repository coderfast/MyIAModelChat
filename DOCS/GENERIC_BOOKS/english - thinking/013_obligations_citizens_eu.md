# Obligations of EU Citizens

## Chapter 1: Contributing to the EU Budget

### 1.1 The EU's Own Resources System

The EU's own resources system constitutes the financial base that enables the Union to finance its policies and programs. European citizens contribute to the Union's budget through different channels, including own resources based on gross national product, resources derived from Value Added Tax, and own resources based on unrecycled plastic waste. Understanding this system is fundamental to understanding the financial obligations of European citizens towards the Union.

The own resources based on gross national product represent the main source of revenue for the Union's budget. This resource is calculated by applying a percentage to the own resources base of each member state, which is determined based on its net national contribution. The percentage varies among member states, as some member states benefit from reductions known as "corrections," which limit their net contribution to the Union's budget. These corrections are subject to debate, as they alter the principle of financial solidarity among member states.

The resources derived from Value Added Tax are another important own resource of the Union's budget. This resource is calculated by applying a harmonized percentage to the VAT base of each member state. The percentage is determined annually based on the financing needs of the Union's budget. The VAT-derived resources contribute to the financing of the Union's budget in proportion to the consumption of each member state, which reflects the principle of financial solidarity.

The own resources based on unrecycled plastic waste are a new own resource that was introduced by Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2020/2053. This resource is calculated by applying a percentage to the weight of unrecycled plastic waste of each member state, with the aim of incentivizing the reduction of plastic waste and promoting the circular economy. The introduction of this own resource reflects the Union's commitment to environmental sustainability and the transition towards a greener economy.

### 1.2 National Contributions to the Budget

National contributions to the Union's budget are the amounts that each member state must contribute annually to finance the Union's policies and programs. These contributions are calculated based on the own resources corresponding to each member state, taking into account the corrections and reductions that may apply. National contributions are an essential component of the Union's financing system and reflect the financial solidarity among member states.

National contributions to the Union's budget are managed by national governments, which are responsible for collecting and transferring the funds to the European Commission. National governments use different mechanisms to collect the funds, including VAT collection, customs duties collection, and other taxes. The efficient management of national contributions is fundamental to ensuring the financial stability of the Union's budget and to financing the Union's policies and programs.

European citizens indirectly contribute to the Union's budget through national taxes that finance member states' contributions. Although citizens do not pay taxes directly to the Union, their national tax contributions are the main source of financing for the Union's budget. This indirect financing system reflects the intergovernmental nature of the Union's budget and the importance of financial solidarity among member states.

National contributions to the Union's budget are subject to audits and controls by the European Court of Auditors and national audit bodies. These controls ensure that the Union's funds are used efficiently and effectively, and that national contributions are calculated and transferred correctly. Transparency and accountability in the management of national contributions are fundamental to citizens' confidence in the Union's financing system.

### 1.3 EU Taxes and Levies

The European Union has the competence to establish certain taxes and levies in specific areas, such as external trade, agriculture, and fisheries policy. These taxes and levies are collected by member states and transferred to the European Commission to finance the Union's policies and programs. European citizens may be subject to these taxes and levies in certain circumstances, such as when importing goods from third countries or when using agricultural products subsidized by the Union.

Customs duties are the most important taxes collected by the Union in the area of external trade. Customs duties are applied to goods imported from third countries and vary depending on the type of goods, their origin, and their value. Customs duties are an important source of revenue for the Union's budget and contribute to the financing of the Union's trade and agricultural policies. European citizens who import goods from third countries are subject to the payment of the corresponding customs duties.

Veterinary and phytosanitary levies are other taxes collected by the Union in the area of food safety. These levies are applied to the importation of food products from third countries and aim to ensure food safety and the protection of public health. Veterinary and phytosanitary levies are collected by member states and transferred to the European Commission to finance the Union's food safety policies.

Contributions to the European Agricultural Guarantee Fund (EAGF) are another way in which European citizens contribute to the Union's budget in the agricultural area. The EAGF finances direct payments to European farmers and rural development programs. Contributions to the EAGF are calculated based on the agricultural production of each member state and are transferred to the Union's budget to finance the Union's agricultural policies.

### 1.4 Transparency and Accountability

Transparency and accountability are fundamental principles in the management of the Union's budget. The Union has established mechanisms to ensure that the Union's funds are used efficiently, effectively, and transparently, and that citizens can follow the use of their contributions. These mechanisms include auditing by the European Court of Auditors, supervision by the European Parliament, and publication of budgetary information.

The European Court of Auditors is the external audit body of the Union. Its mission is to verify that the Union's revenue and expenditure are carried out in accordance with European legislation and that the Union's funds are used efficiently and effectively. The Court of Auditors presents annual reports to the European Parliament and the Council on the management of the Union's budget. These reports are public and allow citizens to follow the use of their contributions to the Union's budget.

The European Parliament exercises a supervisory function over the Union's budget. The Parliament approves the Union's annual budget and supervises its execution through the Committee on Budgets. The Parliament also approves the statement of assurance of the High Representative for the budget, which certifies that the EEAS expenditures have been carried out in accordance with the approved budget. This supervisory function is fundamental to transparency and accountability in the management of the Union's budget.

The publication of budgetary information is another mechanism of transparency and accountability. The European Commission publishes annual reports on the execution of the Union's budget, which include detailed information on revenue and expenditure. These reports are available on the European Commission's website and allow citizens to follow the use of their contributions to the Union's budget. Transparency and accountability are fundamental to citizens' confidence in the Union's financing system.

## Chapter 2: Respecting the EU Legal Order

### 2.1 The Principle of Primacy of EU Law

The principle of primacy of EU law is a fundamental principle of the Union's legal order that establishes the supremacy of EU law over the national law of member states. This principle means that, in case of conflict between EU law and national law, EU law prevails over national law. The principle of primacy has been recognized by the Court of Justice of the European Union since its judgment in the Costa v ENEL case in 1964 and has been confirmed by consistent case law ever since.

The principle of primacy applies to all sources of EU law, including the Treaties, regulations, directives, and the case law of the Court of Justice. European citizens have the obligation to respect EU law in all its manifestations, including European legislation, the case law of the Court of Justice, and the acts of the Union's institutions. Respect for EU law is a fundamental obligation of European citizens and a prerequisite for the proper functioning of the Union.

The principle of primacy has important implications for European citizens. First, citizens have the right to invoke EU law before national courts to protect their rights. Second, citizens have the obligation to comply with EU law in all its manifestations. Third, member states have the obligation to ensure the effectiveness of EU law in their territory, which includes the obligation not to adopt measures that are contrary to EU law.

The principle of primacy is not absolute and is subject to certain limitations. The Court of Justice has recognized that the principle of primacy must be compatible with the fundamental principles of national legal orders, such as the protection of fundamental rights and the principle of legal certainty. The Court of Justice has also established that the principle of primacy does not apply retroactively and that citizens cannot be sanctioned for acts that were in compliance with national law at the time they were carried out, but that are contrary to EU law.

### 2.2 Citizens' Obligations Under EU Law

European citizens have a series of obligations under EU law that derive from their status as citizens of the Union. These obligations include respect for European legislation, compliance with internal market rules, declaration of capital movements, and cooperation with European and national authorities. Non-compliance with these obligations may result in administrative, civil, or criminal sanctions, depending on the nature and severity of the infringement.

Respect for European legislation is the fundamental obligation of European citizens. Citizens have the obligation to comply with all provisions of EU law that are applicable to them, including provisions of the Treaties, regulations, directives, and the case law of the Court of Justice. Non-compliance with European legislation may result in sanctions that vary depending on the nature of the infringement, from administrative fines to prison sentences in the most serious cases.

Compliance with internal market rules is another important obligation of European citizens. The internal market is based on the free movement of persons, goods, services, and capital, and citizens have the obligation to respect the rules governing these freedoms. These rules include rules of origin, competition rules, consumer protection rules, and environmental protection rules. Non-compliance with internal market rules may result in sanctions that include fines, prohibition of economic activities, and civil liability for damages caused.

The declaration of capital movements is another obligation of European citizens. Citizens have the obligation to declare to the competent authorities capital movements that exceed certain thresholds, with the aim of preventing money laundering and terrorist financing. This obligation is established in European legislation on the prevention of money laundering and applies to all natural and legal persons who carry out capital movements in the Union.

### 2.3 Sanctions for Non-Compliance

Sanctions for non-compliance with EU law are the legal consequences that arise from non-compliance with the obligations established in European legislation. Sanctions vary depending on the nature and severity of the infringement, and may be administrative, civil, or criminal. Sanctions aim to ensure compliance with EU law and protect the interests of the Union and its citizens.

Administrative sanctions are imposed by national or European administrative authorities for non-compliance with European administrative rules. These sanctions include fines, revocation of licenses, prohibition of economic activities, and other restrictive measures. Administrative sanctions are particularly important in areas such as competition, environmental protection, food safety, and personal data protection.

Civil sanctions are imposed by national or European courts for non-compliance with civil obligations established in European legislation. These sanctions include compensation for damages, annulment of contracts, and other civil measures. Civil sanctions are particularly important in areas such as consumer protection, liability for damages caused by defective products, and protection of intellectual property rights.

Criminal sanctions are imposed by national courts for the commission of offenses established in European legislation. These sanctions include prison sentences, criminal fines, and other penalties. Criminal sanctions are particularly important in areas such as the fight against fraud, corruption, terrorism, human trafficking, and drug trafficking. The Union has adopted a series of directives that establish minimum rules on criminal sanctions in these areas, with the aim of ensuring effective protection of EU law in all member states.

### 2.4 Cooperation with Authorities

Cooperation with European and national authorities is a fundamental obligation of European citizens that derives from their status as citizens of the Union. Citizens have the obligation to cooperate with authorities in the investigation and prosecution of infringements of EU law, providing the requested information, facilitating access to documents, and participating in administrative and judicial proceedings. Cooperation with authorities is essential to ensure the effectiveness of EU law and the protection of the interests of the Union and its citizens.

The obligation to provide information to authorities is one of the most common forms of cooperation. Citizens have the obligation to provide authorities with the information requested in relation to compliance with EU law. This information includes financial data, tax data, data on economic activities, and other relevant data. Non-compliance with this obligation may result in administrative, civil, or criminal sanctions.

The obligation to facilitate access to documents is another form of cooperation. Citizens have the obligation to facilitate authorities' access to documents requested in relation to the investigation and prosecution of infringements of EU law. This obligation extends to physical and electronic documents, and applies both to documents in the citizen's possession and to documents that the citizen may obtain from third parties. Non-compliance with this obligation may result in sanctions.

Participation in administrative and judicial proceedings is another form of cooperation. Citizens have the obligation to participate in administrative and judicial proceedings in which they are required, providing the requested information, appearing before authorities, and respecting the decisions adopted. Participation in proceedings is fundamental to the effectiveness of EU law and to the protection of citizens' rights.

## Chapter 3: Participating in Elections

### 3.1 Obligation to Participate in European Elections

Participation in European elections is a right and a responsibility of European citizens. Although most member states do not impose a legal obligation to vote, participation in elections is considered a fundamental civic duty that contributes to the legitimacy and effectiveness of the European Parliament. The European Parliament is the only Union institution directly elected by citizens, and its legitimacy depends on citizens' participation in elections.

Participation in European elections has historically been low compared to national elections, which has raised concerns about the legitimacy of the European Parliament. The Union has adopted measures to promote participation in European elections, such as simplifying voting procedures for citizens residing in another member state, promoting civic education, and organizing awareness campaigns. These measures aim to increase participation in European elections and strengthen the democratic legitimacy of the European Parliament.

Participation in European elections also implies participation in the democratic life of the Union. European citizens have the opportunity to influence the Union's decision-making through their vote, as the European Parliament plays an important role in the adoption of European legislation and in the supervision of the Union's other institutions. Participation in elections is therefore an important way to exercise European citizenship and contribute to the functioning of the Union.

The Union has adopted measures to facilitate participation in European elections for citizens residing in another member state. The Treaty of Lisbon establishes the right of Union citizens to vote and stand as candidates in European and municipal elections in the member state where they reside, regardless of their nationality. These provisions have been developed by European directives that establish the rules for exercising this right, including registration procedures and voting arrangements.

### 3.2 Obligation to Participate in Municipal Elections

Participation in municipal elections is another civic responsibility of European citizens residing in a member state other than that of their nationality. The Treaty of Lisbon establishes the right of Union citizens to vote and stand as candidates in municipal elections in the member state where they reside, regardless of their nationality. This right has been developed by European directives that establish the rules for exercising this right.

Participation in municipal elections is particularly important for European citizens who have established their lives in another member state. Municipal elections directly affect citizens' quality of life, as municipalities are responsible for essential services such as education, healthcare, transport, and housing. Participation in municipal elections allows citizens to influence the management of these services and decision-making that directly affects their daily lives.

The Union has adopted measures to facilitate participation in municipal elections for citizens residing in another member state. The registration procedure as voters in the member state of residence is relatively straightforward and is usually carried out through the municipal authorities. Citizens must provide proof of their nationality and residence in the municipality, and once registered, they may vote in municipal elections under the same conditions as nationals.

Participation in municipal elections also implies participation in the democratic life of the municipality. European citizens residing in a member state other than that of their nationality can participate in the democratic life of the municipality through various channels, such as participating in town council meetings, submitting proposals, and participating in public consultations. This participation is fundamental to the integration of citizens in the local community and to the promotion of social cohesion.

### 3.3 Obligations During the Electoral Campaign

European citizens have a series of obligations during the electoral campaign that are established in European and national legislation. These obligations include respect for political party financing rules, respect for electoral advertising rules, and respect for campaign conduct rules. Non-compliance with these obligations may result in administrative or criminal sanctions.

Respect for political party financing rules is a fundamental obligation during the electoral campaign. Political parties participating in European and municipal elections must comply with the financing rules established by European and national legislation. These rules include limiting campaign expenditure, the obligation to declare income and expenditure, and the prohibition of receiving financing from foreign sources. Non-compliance with these rules may result in the annulment of electoral results and criminal sanctions.

Respect for electoral advertising rules is another obligation during the campaign. Candidates and political parties must comply with the electoral advertising rules established by European and national legislation. These rules include the obligation to identify electoral advertising, the prohibition of unfair use of public media, and respect for the rights of other candidates. Non-compliance with these rules may result in administrative sanctions.

Respect for campaign conduct rules is another important obligation. Candidates and citizens must respect the conduct rules during the campaign, which include the prohibition of violence, intimidation, and discrimination, and respect for the rights of other candidates and citizens. Non-compliance with these rules may result in criminal sanctions and the annulment of electoral results.

### 3.4 Participation in Consultations and Democratic Processes

Participation in consultations and democratic processes is another obligation and opportunity for European citizens to contribute to the Union's decision-making. The Union has established a series of mechanisms to promote citizen participation, such as public consultations, participatory legislative processes, and inclusive decision-making processes. Participation in these mechanisms is fundamental to the legitimacy and effectiveness of the Union's decisions.

Public consultations are a mechanism for gathering citizens' opinions on public policy issues. The European Commission conducts public consultations on legislative proposals, strategies, and other public policy issues. Citizens can participate in public consultations through the European Commission's online platform, providing their opinions and suggestions. Public consultations are an important mechanism for citizen participation and for incorporating citizens' opinions into the Union's decision-making.

Participatory legislative processes are mechanisms for citizen participation in the adoption of European legislation. The Union has established several mechanisms for citizen participation in the legislative process, such as the European Citizens' Initiative, which allows one million citizens from at least seven member states to request the European Commission to submit a legislative proposal. Participatory legislative processes are an important mechanism for citizen participation and for the democratic legitimacy of European legislation.

Participation in inclusive decision-making processes is another mechanism of citizen participation. The Union has established several mechanisms to promote citizen participation in decision-making, such as the European Economic and Social Committee and the Committee of the Regions, which represent the interests of citizens and local and regional authorities. These mechanisms are fundamental to citizen participation and to incorporating citizens' opinions into the Union's decision-making.

## Chapter 4: Military Service and Defense

### 4.1 Military Service in Member States

Military service is an obligation that varies significantly among the member states of the European Union. While some member states maintain compulsory military service, others have adopted professional army systems. The decision to maintain or abolish compulsory military service is the exclusive competence of each member state, and the European Union does not have the competence to impose a single defense model. However, defense is an area of growing cooperation among member states through the Common Security and Defence Policy.

Member states that maintain compulsory military service include Austria, Cyprus, Greece, and Finland, among others. In these countries, male citizens are obliged to perform military service for a period that varies between six months and one year, depending on the country. Compulsory military service aims to guarantee national defense and the country's security, as well as to promote values of citizenship, discipline, and responsibility among young people. Some member states offer the alternative of civil service for those who oppose military service on grounds of conscience.

Member states with professional armies include France, Germany, Italy, Spain, and the Netherlands, among others. In these countries, citizens are not obliged to perform military service but may voluntarily join the armed forces. Professional armies are based on voluntary recruitment and offer long-term professional careers in the armed forces. The trend in the European Union is towards the professionalization of the armed forces, but some member states have maintained compulsory military service for reasons of national security.

Defense cooperation among member states is increasingly important in the context of the Common Security and Defence Policy. Member states cooperate in areas such as military training, defense research, peacekeeping operations, and crisis management. This cooperation takes place through mechanisms such as Permanent Structured Cooperation, the European Peace Facility, and the European Defence Agency. Defense cooperation allows member states to share resources and capabilities to improve their effectiveness in security matters.

### 4.2 Citizens' Defense Obligations

European citizens have a series of defense obligations that vary depending on the national legislation of each member state. These obligations include national defense, cooperation with military authorities, and participation in civil defense exercises. The scope and nature of these obligations depend on whether the member state maintains compulsory military service or has a professional army.

National defense is the fundamental obligation of citizens in defense matters. In member states with compulsory military service, male citizens are obliged to perform military or civil service for a specified period. In member states with professional armies, citizens have no obligation to perform military service but may be called up in situations of national emergency or armed conflict. National defense is the responsibility of all citizens and is fundamental to the country's security and sovereignty.

Cooperation with military authorities is another citizens' obligation in defense matters. Citizens have the obligation to cooperate with military authorities when required, providing the requested information, facilitating access to their property, and participating in civil defense exercises. Non-compliance with this obligation may result in administrative or criminal sanctions, depending on national legislation.

Participation in civil defense exercises is another citizens' obligation in defense matters. Citizens may be called to participate in civil defense exercises that aim to prepare the population for emergency situations, such as natural disasters, armed conflicts, or terrorist attacks. These exercises are organized by national authorities and aim to improve the population's preparedness for emergency situations.

### 4.3 Civil Service and Conscientious Objection

Civil service is an alternative to compulsory military service that is available in member states that maintain compulsory military service. Civil service allows citizens who oppose military service on grounds of conscience to serve in areas such as healthcare, education, environmental protection, or social assistance. The right to conscientious objection is recognized by the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union and by the national legislation of most member states.

The right to conscientious objection is a fundamental right that protects citizens' freedom of conscience. Citizens applying for civil service must demonstrate that their objection to military service is based on moral, ethical, religious, or other deeply held convictions. National authorities evaluate conscientious objection applications and, if approved, assign citizens to civil service for a period that is generally longer than military service.

Civil service aims to contribute to the well-being of society and the provision of essential services to the population. Citizens performing civil service work in a variety of areas, such as healthcare, education, environmental protection, social assistance, and civil protection. Civil service is a way to contribute to society and fulfill the defense obligation without bearing arms or participating in military operations.

Conscientious objection is not without controversy. Some argue that conscientious objection weakens national defense by reducing the number of people available for military service. Others argue that the right to conscientious objection is a fundamental right that must be protected, as freedom of conscience is an essential democratic value. The debate on conscientious objection reflects the tension between the obligation of national defense and the protection of citizens' fundamental rights.

### 4.4 Contribution to European Defense

Contribution to European defense is a growing responsibility of European citizens in the context of the Common Security and Defence Policy. Although defense is the competence of member states, the Union has established mechanisms for defense cooperation that require citizen participation. Contribution to European defense is made through different channels, including service in national armed forces participating in Union missions, participation in European training programs, and financial contribution to Union defense instruments.

Service in national armed forces participating in Union missions is one of the most direct ways to contribute to European defense. European citizens serving in the armed forces of member states may be called to participate in Union missions, such as military missions in the Balkans, Africa, and the Middle East. These missions aim to contribute to international peace and security, and citizens' participation in these missions is a direct contribution to European defense.

Participation in European training programs is another way to contribute to European defense. The Union has established military training programs, such as the European Union Training Mission in Mali, which aims to train local security forces. European citizens participating in these programs contribute to the training of security forces and peace consolidation in conflict-affected regions.

Financial contribution to Union defense instruments is another way to contribute to European defense. European citizens contribute to Union defense instruments through their national taxes, which finance member states' contributions to the Union's budget. The European Peace Facility, for example, finances research, development, and acquisition of military capabilities, and member states contribute to this fund through their national budgets, which are financed by citizens' taxes.

## Chapter 5: Solidarity Duties

### 5.1 Solidarity Among Member States

Solidarity among member states is a fundamental principle of the European Union that is reflected in a series of obligations of citizens and member states. Solidarity implies that member states and their citizens have the responsibility to support each other in situations of need, sharing resources and burdens equitably. This principle is enshrined in the Treaties and has been developed through a series of legal instruments and policies.

Financial solidarity is one of the most important manifestations of solidarity among member states. European citizens contribute to the Union's budget through their national taxes, and these funds are used to finance policies and programs that benefit all member states, particularly the most disadvantaged. Financial solidarity is manifested in instruments such as the Cohesion Fund, which supports the development of less developed member states, and the European Social Fund, which finances employment and training programs.

Security solidarity is another important manifestation of solidarity among member states. Member states have the obligation to come to the aid of another member state that is the victim of armed aggression or a natural disaster, in accordance with Article 42.7 of the Treaty on European Union and Article 222 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. These provisions establish the obligation of mutual assistance in security matters and reflect member states' commitment to protect each other.

Migration solidarity is another important manifestation of solidarity among member states. Member states have the obligation to share the burden of migration equitably, through mechanisms such as the relocation mechanism and the solidarity mechanism. These mechanisms aim to ensure that the burden of migration is distributed equitably among member states and that member states facing migration pressures receive support from other member states.

### 5.2 Citizen Solidarity Duties

Citizen solidarity duties are obligations of European citizens that derive from solidarity among member states and among citizens. These duties include assistance in emergency situations, participation in volunteering programs, contribution to social cohesion, and respect for the rights of other citizens. Citizen solidarity duties are fundamental to social cohesion and to the functioning of the Union.

Assistance in emergency situations is a citizen solidarity duty that is manifested in citizens' obligation to help persons in danger, in accordance with national legislation. In some member states, such as France, Italy, and Belgium, failure to render assistance is a criminal offense punishable by law. This solidarity duty is fundamental to the protection of life and personal integrity, and reflects the values of humanity and solidarity that inspire the European project.

Participation in volunteering programs is another form of citizen solidarity. European citizens can participate in volunteering programs that aim to contribute to the well-being of the community, such as helping elderly people, assisting persons with disabilities, environmental protection, and education. Volunteering programs are promoted by the European Union through initiatives such as the European Solidarity Corps, which offers young people the opportunity to volunteer in different Union countries.

Contribution to social cohesion is another citizen solidarity duty. European citizens have the responsibility to contribute to the social cohesion of their communities, respecting the rights of other citizens, promoting cultural diversity, and combating discrimination. Social cohesion is fundamental to the functioning of the Union and to building a more just and equitable society.

### 5.3 Solidarity Duties in Crisis Situations

Solidarity duties in crisis situations are obligations of European citizens that are activated in emergency situations, such as natural disasters, pandemics, or armed conflicts. These duties include cooperation with authorities, assistance to affected persons, and contribution to crisis management. Solidarity duties in crisis situations are fundamental to the protection of life and personal integrity, and to the recovery of affected communities.

Cooperation with authorities in crisis situations is a fundamental duty of citizens. Citizens have the obligation to cooperate with authorities in crisis management, providing the requested information, following authorities' instructions, and participating in civil protection measures. Cooperation with authorities is essential to the effectiveness of crisis response measures and to the protection of life and personal integrity.

Assistance to affected persons is another solidarity duty in crisis situations. European citizens have the responsibility to help persons affected by a crisis, providing material, psychological, and emotional assistance. This assistance can be provided individually or through volunteering and relief organizations. Assistance to affected persons is fundamental to mitigating the effects of the crisis and to the recovery of affected communities.

Contribution to crisis management is another citizen solidarity duty. Citizens can contribute to crisis management in various ways, such as participating in search and rescue tasks, donating blood, donating food and other basic goods, and participating in cleanup and reconstruction tasks. Citizens' contribution to crisis management is fundamental to the recovery of affected communities and the reconstruction of damaged infrastructure.

### 5.4 Solidarity with Refugees and Asylum Seekers

Solidarity with refugees and asylum seekers is a citizen solidarity duty that has become an issue of growing importance in the European Union. Refugees and asylum seekers are persons who have fled their country of origin due to armed conflicts, persecution, or human rights violations, and who seek protection in the European Union. European citizens have the responsibility to show solidarity with these persons and to contribute to their protection and integration.

Protection of refugees and asylum seekers is an international obligation of the European Union and its member states. The Union has adopted a legal framework for the protection of refugees, which includes the Reception Conditions Directive, the Procedures Directive, and the Qualification Directive. These directives establish minimum standards for the reception, procedures, and qualification of asylum seekers, and ensure that refugees receive adequate protection in the Union.

Integration of refugees is another important aspect of solidarity with refugees. The European Union promotes the integration of refugees through initiatives such as the Asylum, Migration and Integration Fund, which finances integration projects in member states. European citizens can contribute to the integration of refugees by participating in volunteering programs, supporting integration initiatives, and promoting cultural diversity in their communities.

Solidarity with refugees is also manifested in the reception of refugees by European communities and families. Some member states have established refugee reception programs that allow European families to host refugees in their homes. These programs are a concrete form of solidarity that allows European citizens to contribute directly to the protection and integration of refugees.

## Chapter 6: Tax Obligations

### 6.1 Tax Obligations in the Union

Tax obligations of European citizens are a fundamental aspect of their relationship with the European Union and with member states. Although taxation is the competence of member states, the Union has established European rules that affect citizens' tax obligations, particularly in areas such as VAT, income tax, and tax fraud prevention. Understanding these obligations is fundamental to compliance with the law and to contributing to the functioning of the Union.

Value Added Tax (VAT) is a European tax applied to most commercial transactions in the Union. European citizens are subject to VAT when acquiring goods and services, and businesses are obliged to collect and remit VAT. The Union has established harmonized rules for VAT, including the tax rate, exemptions, and refund rules. Compliance with VAT rules is a fundamental obligation of business citizens and contributes to the financing of national and Union budgets.

Income tax is the competence of member states, but the Union has adopted measures to combat tax evasion and cross-border tax avoidance. These measures include the Directive on Administrative Cooperation in the Field of Taxation, which establishes information exchange mechanisms between the tax authorities of member states, and the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, which establishes rules to combat tax evasion and cross-border tax avoidance. European citizens have the obligation to declare and pay income tax in the state where they reside or where they earn their income.

Tax fraud prevention is another obligation of European citizens. The Union has adopted a series of measures to combat tax fraud, including the Directive on Administrative Cooperation in the Field of Taxation, which establishes information exchange mechanisms between the tax authorities of member states, and the Regulation on the general rules for information and communication on cryptocurrencies, which establishes information obligations for cryptocurrency service providers. European citizens have the obligation to comply with tax fraud prevention rules and to declare their income and assets in accordance with national and European legislation.

### 6.2 Combating Tax Fraud and Evasion

Combating tax fraud and evasion is a priority of the European Union that directly affects citizens' tax obligations. Tax fraud and cross-border tax evasion rob member states of billions of euros annually in tax revenue, reducing the resources available for financing public services and Union policies. The Union has adopted a series of measures to combat tax fraud and evasion, including harmonization of tax rules, information exchange between tax authorities, and international cooperation.

Harmonization of tax rules is a fundamental measure to combat tax fraud and cross-border tax avoidance. The Union has adopted directives that establish common rules for VAT, income tax, and other taxes, with the aim of reducing differences between national tax systems that may be exploited for tax fraud and tax avoidance. Harmonization of tax rules is particularly important in the internal market, where the free movement of persons, goods, services, and capital can facilitate cross-border tax fraud.

Information exchange between tax authorities is another fundamental measure to combat tax fraud and evasion. The Union has adopted the Directive on Administrative Cooperation in the Field of Taxation, which establishes automatic information exchange mechanisms between the tax authorities of member states. Automatic information exchange allows tax authorities to detect cross-border transactions that have not been declared by citizens and to combat tax fraud and cross-border tax evasion.

International cooperation is another fundamental measure to combat tax fraud and cross-border tax evasion. The Union has established international cooperation agreements in tax matters with third countries, including information exchange and mutual assistance in tax collection. International cooperation is particularly important to combat tax fraud and tax evasion in tax havens, which are jurisdictions that offer favorable tax conditions for tax avoidance.

### 6.3 Contribution to Public Services

Contribution to public services is a tax obligation of European citizens that translates into the payment of national taxes that finance public services. Public services include education, healthcare, security, infrastructure, and other essential services provided by the state. European citizens have the obligation to contribute to the financing of these services through the payment of taxes, and have the right to benefit from these services on an equal basis.

The financing of public services is carried out through a combination of direct and indirect taxes. Direct taxes, such as income tax and wealth tax, are paid directly by citizens based on their income and wealth. Indirect taxes, such as VAT and excise duties, are paid by citizens when they acquire goods and services. The combination of direct and indirect taxes provides the necessary financing for public services and ensures that the tax burden is distributed equitably among citizens.

Contribution to public services also implies respect for tax rules and timely payment of taxes. European citizens have the obligation to declare and pay their taxes in accordance with national and European legislation, and to cooperate with tax authorities in the audit and collection of taxes. The Union has adopted the NIS2 Directive (Security of Networks and Information Systems), which establishes cybersecurity obligations for essential and important entities. Cybersecurity is fundamental to the protection of citizens' fundamental rights and to the security of the Union.

Safety of products and services is another fundamental obligation of businesses. Businesses have the obligation to ensure that the products and services they offer are safe and do not pose a risk to the health and safety of consumers. Businesses must notify the competent authorities of dangerous products and adopt the necessary measures to withdraw from the market products that pose a risk to the health and safety of consumers. Safety of products and services is fundamental to the protection of consumers' fundamental rights.

## Chapter 14: Digital Area Obligations

### 14.1 Citizens' Obligations in the Digital Space

European citizens have a series of obligations in the digital space that derive from European and national legislation. These obligations include respect for intellectual property rights, protection of personal data, cybersecurity, and prevention of online illicit activities. Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to security and trust in the digital space.

Respect for intellectual property rights is a fundamental obligation of citizens in the digital space. Citizens have the obligation not to download, copy, or distribute content protected by copyright without the authorization of the rights holder. The Union has adopted the Directive on Copyright in the Digital Single Market (DSM Directive), which establishes rules for the protection of copyright in the digital environment. Non-compliance with intellectual property rights may result in civil and criminal sanctions.

Protection of personal data is another fundamental obligation of citizens in the digital space. Citizens have the obligation to respect the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which establishes rules for the processing of personal data. Citizens must protect their own personal data and respect the privacy of others. The GDPR establishes rights for citizens in relation to their personal data, but also establishes obligations that citizens must comply with.

Cybersecurity is another important obligation of citizens in the digital space. Citizens have the obligation to protect their devices and networks against cyber threats, using secure passwords, regularly updating their software, and avoiding insecure practices. Citizens also have the obligation to report cyber threats to the competent authorities and to cooperate in the investigation of cybercrime. Cybersecurity is fundamental to the protection of citizens' fundamental rights and to the security of the digital space.

### 14.2 Digital Platforms' Obligations

Digital platforms have a series of obligations that derive from European legislation, including the Digital Services Act (DSA) and the Digital Markets Act (DMA). These obligations include transparency in content moderation, protection of fundamental rights, and liability for illegal content. Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to security and trust in the digital space.

Transparency in content moderation is a fundamental obligation of digital platforms. Platforms must provide clear information about their content moderation policies, including the criteria used to identify and remove illegal content. Platforms must also inform users about the reasons for content removal and offer appeal mechanisms. Transparency in content moderation is fundamental to the protection of freedom of expression and other fundamental rights.

Protection of fundamental rights is another important obligation of digital platforms. Platforms must carry out fundamental rights impact assessments and adopt measures to mitigate risks to fundamental rights, such as the spread of hate content, disinformation, and electoral interference. Platforms must also ensure that their algorithms do not discriminate against users on the grounds of sex, race, ethnic origin, religion, or other personal characteristics. Protection of fundamental rights is fundamental to the legitimacy and trust in digital platforms.

Liability for illegal content is another fundamental obligation of digital platforms. Platforms have the obligation to remove illegal content quickly and effectively, and to notify the competent authorities about the removal of illegal content. The DSA establishes a liability framework for digital platforms in relation to illegal content, including significant fines for non-compliance. Liability for illegal content is fundamental to the protection of citizens' fundamental rights and to the security of the digital space.

### 14.3 Disinformation Obligations

Combating disinformation is an increasingly important obligation for citizens and digital platforms in the European digital space. Disinformation refers to false or misleading information that is disseminated with the intention of deceiving or manipulating public opinion, and can have a significant impact on public health, democracy, and security. European citizens have the responsibility not to spread disinformation and to verify information before sharing it online.

Citizens have the obligation to be responsible consumers of information, verifying the reliability of sources before sharing them online. The Union has promoted media and information literacy initiatives to help citizens identify disinformation and make informed decisions about the information they consume and share. Verification of information is fundamental to the protection of democracy and to the prevention of manipulation of public opinion.

Digital platforms have the obligation to combat disinformation on their platforms, adopting measures such as verification of advertisers' identity, labelling of sponsored content, and reduction of the spread of false content. The EU Code of Practice on Disinformation establishes commitments by platforms to combat disinformation, and the DSA establishes stricter obligations for platforms in relation to disinformation. Combating disinformation is fundamental to the protection of democracy and to citizens' confidence in online information.

### 14.4 Artificial Intelligence Obligations

Regulation of artificial intelligence is an emerging area of obligations for citizens and businesses in the European Union. The Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act) establishes rules for the regulation of artificial intelligence and the protection of citizens' fundamental rights. Citizens and businesses have the obligation to respect these rules and to use artificial intelligence in a manner that is responsible and respectful of fundamental rights.

Citizens have the obligation to be aware of the risks associated with the use of artificial intelligence systems and to protect their fundamental rights in the context of artificial intelligence. Citizens must inform themselves about how artificial intelligence systems are used in their daily lives, such as recommendation systems, facial recognition systems, and automated decision-making systems. Citizens also have the right to request explanations about automated decisions that affect them and to challenge decisions that they consider contrary to their fundamental rights.

Businesses developing or using artificial intelligence systems have the obligation to comply with the AI Act, which establishes transparency, human oversight, and impact assessment requirements for high-risk artificial intelligence systems. Businesses must carry out impact assessments before marketing high-risk artificial intelligence systems, and must ensure that these systems comply with the transparency and human oversight requirements. Non-compliance with the AI Act may result in significant fines and other sanctions. Regulation of artificial intelligence is fundamental to the protection of citizens' fundamental rights and to the promotion of responsible artificial intelligence.

European citizens have a series of obligations in the area of the Union's foreign policy that derive from their status as citizens of the Union. Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to the effectiveness of the Union's foreign policy and to the promotion of peace, security, and human rights on the international stage.

Respect for international sanctions is a fundamental obligation of European citizens. The Union has adopted sanctions against several countries, groups, and persons in response to violations of international law, attacks on human rights, or threats to international peace and security. European citizens have the obligation to respect the sanctions and not to carry out activities that may circumvent or evade the sanctions. Non-compliance with sanctions may result in criminal sanctions and other legal consequences.

Cooperation with authorities in foreign policy matters is another obligation of citizens. Citizens have the obligation to cooperate with authorities when required in relation to the Union's foreign policy, providing the requested information and respecting the decisions adopted. Cooperation with authorities is fundamental to the effectiveness of the Union's foreign policy and to the protection of the Union's and its citizens' interests.

Contribution to the promotion of European values on the international stage is another obligation of citizens. European citizens have the responsibility to promote the values of human dignity, freedom, equality, and solidarity in their relations with third countries, contributing to building a more just and secure world. Contribution to the promotion of European values is fundamental to the legitimacy and effectiveness of the Union's foreign policy.

### 15.2 Development Aid Obligations

European citizens have a series of obligations in the area of development aid that derive from the Union's commitment to sustainable development and poverty reduction in the world. These obligations include financial contribution to development aid, promotion of fair trade, and awareness of global development issues. Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to the effectiveness of the Union's development policy and to achieving the Sustainable Development Goals.

Financial contribution to development aid is an obligation of European citizens that is exercised through their national taxes, which finance member states' contributions to the Union's budget. The Union and its member states are the world's largest donors of development aid, and European citizens contribute to this financing through their taxes. Financial contribution to development aid is fundamental to the fight against poverty and to the promotion of sustainable development in partner countries.

Promotion of fair trade is another obligation of European citizens. Citizens can contribute to sustainable development by purchasing fair trade products, which guarantee fair prices for producers in developing countries. Promotion of fair trade is fundamental to poverty reduction and to the promotion of sustainable development in partner countries. Citizens also have the responsibility to inform themselves about the production conditions of the products they consume and to support companies that respect human rights and the environment in their supply chains.

Awareness of global development issues is another obligation of citizens. Citizens have the responsibility to inform themselves about global development issues, such as poverty, hunger, diseases, climate change, and armed conflicts, and to promote awareness in their environment. Awareness is fundamental to public support for the Union's development policy and to the mobilization of resources for the fight against poverty and the promotion of sustainable development.

### 15.3 Judicial Cooperation Obligations

European citizens have a series of obligations in the area of judicial cooperation that derive from European legislation on judicial cooperation in civil and criminal matters. These obligations include respect for judicial decisions of other member states, cooperation with judicial authorities of other member states, and contribution to the effectiveness of European judicial cooperation. Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to the effectiveness of the area of freedom, security, and justice.

Respect for judicial decisions of other member states is a fundamental obligation of European citizens. Citizens have the obligation to respect the judicial decisions of the courts of other member states, including civil judgments, criminal judgments, and family law decisions. The Union has established mechanisms for the recognition and enforcement of judicial decisions between member states, such as the Brussels I bis Regulation in civil matters and the Dublin Regulation in criminal matters. Respect for judicial decisions of other member states is fundamental to mutual trust between member states and to the effectiveness of the area of freedom, security, and justice.

Cooperation with judicial authorities of other member states is another obligation of citizens. Citizens have the obligation to cooperate with judicial authorities of other member states when required, providing the requested information, appearing before courts, and respecting the decisions adopted. Cooperation with judicial authorities of other member states is fundamental to the effectiveness of European judicial cooperation and to the protection of citizens' rights.

Contribution to the effectiveness of European judicial cooperation is another obligation of citizens. Citizens can contribute to the effectiveness of European judicial cooperation by informing themselves about their rights and obligations in the area of judicial cooperation, and by using the judicial cooperation mechanisms established by the Union. Citizens' contribution to the effectiveness of European judicial cooperation is fundamental to the consolidation of the area of freedom, security, and justice.

European citizens have a series of public health obligations that derive from European and national legislation. These obligations include contribution to the sustainability of healthcare systems, compliance with hygiene and health safety rules, participation in prevention and vaccination programs, and cooperation with health authorities in crisis situations. Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to the protection of public health and to the efficient functioning of healthcare systems.

This contribution is fundamental to ensuring that healthcare systems can provide quality services to the entire population. Citizens have the responsibility to use healthcare services responsibly, avoiding unnecessary use of services and contributing to the efficiency of the system.

Compliance with hygiene and health safety rules is another important obligation of citizens. Citizens have the obligation to comply with the hygiene and health safety rules established by health authorities, including food hygiene rules, personal hygiene rules, and rules for protection against infectious diseases. Compliance with these rules is fundamental to disease prevention and to the protection of public health.

Participation in prevention and vaccination programs is another obligation of citizens. Citizens have the responsibility to participate in the prevention and vaccination programs established by health authorities, such as childhood vaccination programs, cancer screening programs, and chronic disease prevention programs. European citizens' obligations in pandemic situations are particularly important for the protection of public health and for the management of the health crisis. Respect for prevention measures is another fundamental obligation in pandemic situations. Contribution to pandemic management is another obligation of citizens. Citizens' contribution to pandemic management is fundamental to the protection of public health and to the recovery of society.

European businesses have a series of public health obligations that derive from European and national legislation. Protection of workers' health is a fundamental obligation of businesses. Businesses have the obligation to guarantee the health and safety of their workers, adopting occupational risk prevention measures, providing personal protective equipment, and training workers in safety matters. Protection of workers' health is fundamental to the prevention of occupational diseases and to the guarantee of decent and safe working conditions.

Safety of products and services is another important obligation of businesses in public health matters. Businesses have the obligation to ensure that the products and services they offer are safe and do not pose a risk to the health of consumers. Safety of products and services is fundamental to the protection of public health.

Notification of disease outbreaks is another obligation of businesses. Businesses that detect disease outbreaks in their facilities or among their workers have the obligation to notify health authorities and adopt the necessary measures to contain the spread of the disease. Notification of disease outbreaks is fundamental to the prevention of epidemics and to the protection of public health.

## Chapter 17: Cultural Heritage Protection Obligations

### 17.1 Citizens' Cultural Heritage Obligations

European citizens have a series of cultural heritage protection obligations that derive from European and national legislation. Cultural heritage includes real property, movable property, intangible assets, and digital assets that have historical, artistic, archaeological, scientific, or cultural value. Citizens have the obligation to respect and protect cultural heritage, which is a fundamental element of European identity and cultural diversity.

Respect for cultural heritage is a fundamental obligation of citizens. Citizens have the obligation not to damage, destroy, or deteriorate cultural heritage property, and to respect the protection rules established by European and national legislation. Cultural heritage is a non-renewable resource that has incalculable value for society, and citizens have the responsibility to contribute to its preservation for future generations.

Participation in cultural heritage protection is another obligation of citizens. Citizens can contribute to cultural heritage protection in various ways, such as participation in volunteering programs for the restoration of heritage property, reporting acts of vandalism against heritage property, and promoting awareness of the importance of cultural heritage. Citizens' participation in cultural heritage protection is fundamental to the preservation of European cultural diversity.

Contribution to the financing of cultural heritage protection is another obligation of citizens. Citizens contribute to the financing of cultural heritage protection through their national taxes, which finance national and regional governments' cultural heritage protection programs. Citizens can also contribute to the financing of cultural heritage protection through donations to heritage protection organizations and participation in patronage programs.

### 17.2 Businesses' Cultural Heritage Obligations

European businesses have a series of cultural heritage protection obligations that derive from European and national legislation. These obligations include protection of heritage property during construction works, notification of archaeological discoveries, and contribution to the financing of cultural heritage protection. Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to the preservation of cultural heritage and to ensuring that economic activities are compatible with heritage protection.

Protection of heritage property during construction works is a fundamental obligation of businesses carrying out construction works in areas with cultural heritage. Businesses must adopt measures to protect heritage property during works, such as carrying out prior archaeological studies, adopting protection measures during works, and notifying the competent authorities of any archaeological discovery. Protection of heritage property during construction works is fundamental to the preservation of cultural heritage.

Notification of archaeological discoveries is another important obligation of businesses. Businesses that discover archaeological remains during their activities have the obligation to notify the competent authorities and to stop activities until the authorities assess the importance of the discovery and adopt the necessary protection measures. Notification of archaeological discoveries is fundamental to the preservation of archaeological heritage and to the knowledge of European history.

Contribution to the financing of cultural heritage protection is another obligation of businesses. Businesses can contribute to the financing of cultural heritage protection through donations to heritage protection organizations, participation in patronage programs, and compliance with tax obligations established by national legislation. Businesses' contribution to the financing of cultural heritage protection is fundamental to the preservation of heritage and to ensuring that economic activities contribute to the cultural well-being of society.

## Chapter 18: Education and Training Obligations

### 18.1 Citizens' Educational Obligations

European citizens have a series of educational obligations that derive from European and national legislation. These obligations include compulsory schooling, participation in lifelong learning, and respect for educational rules. Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to the development of European human capital and to the promotion of equal opportunities.

Compulsory schooling is a fundamental obligation of parents and legal guardians. Parents have the obligation to ensure that their children receive quality education during the compulsory schooling period, which generally covers from age 6 to 16. Compulsory schooling is fundamental to the development of citizens' basic competences and to their integration in society. The Union promotes access to quality education for all citizens, regardless of their social, economic, or cultural background.

Participation in lifelong learning is another obligation of citizens. Citizens have the responsibility to maintain and improve their competences throughout their lives, adapting to changes in the labor market and in society. Lifelong learning is fundamental to citizens' employability and to their active participation in society. The Union has established the Principle of Lifelong Learning, which promotes lifelong learning as a right and a responsibility of citizens.

Respect for educational rules is another obligation of citizens. Citizens have the obligation to respect the rules of educational institutions, to participate actively in the learning process, and to contribute to creating a positive educational environment. Respect for educational rules is fundamental to the effectiveness of the educational process and to peaceful coexistence in educational institutions.

### 18.2 Educational Institutions' Obligations

European educational institutions have a series of obligations that derive from European and national legislation. These obligations include guaranteeing quality education, promoting educational inclusion, protecting students' rights, and cooperating with families. Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to the effectiveness of the educational system and to the promotion of equal opportunities.

Guaranteeing quality education is a fundamental obligation of educational institutions. Educational institutions must provide education that develops the competences, knowledge, and attitudes necessary for active participation in society and in the labor market. Quality education includes access to adequate educational resources, teacher training, and continuous evaluation of the educational process. The Union has established reference frameworks for education, such as the European Qualifications Framework, which promotes the quality and comparability of educational qualifications.

Promoting educational inclusion is another important obligation of educational institutions. Educational institutions must ensure that all students have access to quality education, regardless of their social, economic, or cultural background, their sex, their disability, or any other personal characteristic. Educational inclusion includes adapting teaching methods to students' needs, providing additional support to students with learning difficulties, and promoting cultural diversity in the classroom.

Protection of students' rights is another obligation of educational institutions. Educational institutions must ensure that students enjoy their fundamental rights, including the right to privacy, the right to personal data protection, and the right to freedom of expression. Educational institutions must also adopt measures to prevent and combat bullying, discrimination, and violence in the educational environment.

### 18.3 Citizens' Contribution to Educational Improvement

European citizens have the responsibility to contribute to improving the educational system, participating actively in the educational life of their children and their community. This contribution is manifested in participation in parent associations, collaboration with educational institutions, promotion of reading and learning at home, and support for community educational initiatives.

Participation in parent associations is an important way to contribute to educational improvement. Parents can participate in parent associations to express their opinions about their children's education, to collaborate with educational institutions, and to promote educational quality improvement. Parent associations play an important role in representing families' interests and in promoting parental participation in educational life.

Collaboration with educational institutions is another way to contribute to educational improvement. Parents and citizens can collaborate with educational institutions in various ways, such as participation in educational activities, donation of educational resources, and promotion of improvement of educational facilities. Collaboration between families and educational institutions is fundamental to the effectiveness of the educational process and to the promotion of quality education.

Promotion of reading and learning at home is another way to contribute to educational improvement. Parents have the responsibility to promote reading and learning at home, providing their children with an enriching environment that fosters the development of linguistic, cognitive, and social competences. Promotion of reading and learning at home is fundamental to children's educational success and to their comprehensive development.

## Chapter 19: Building European Citizenship

### 19.1 Commitment to European Identity

European citizens have the responsibility to build and maintain a shared European identity that transcends national, cultural, and linguistic differences. European identity is based on common values such as human dignity, freedom, equality, solidarity, and respect for the law, and is manifested in participation in European democratic life, respect for cultural diversity, and promotion of intercultural dialogue. Commitment to European identity is fundamental to social cohesion and to the legitimacy of the European project.

Participation in European democratic life is a fundamental manifestation of commitment to European identity. Citizens can participate in European democratic life through voting in European elections, participation in the European Citizens' Initiative, submission of petitions to the European Parliament, and participation in public consultations. Participation in European democratic life is fundamental to the legitimacy of European institutions and to building a shared European identity.

Respect for cultural diversity is another manifestation of commitment to European identity. European citizens have the responsibility to respect and value the cultural, linguistic, and religious diversity of the Union, which is one of the main strengths of the European project. Respect for cultural diversity includes learning languages, participating in cultural activities, and promoting intercultural dialogue.

Promotion of intercultural dialogue is another manifestation of commitment to European identity. European citizens have the responsibility to promote intercultural dialogue in their communities, contributing to mutual understanding among persons of different cultural, linguistic, and religious backgrounds. Intercultural dialogue is fundamental to social cohesion and to building a pluralistic and tolerant society.

### 19.2 Participation in Building the Union

European citizens have the responsibility to participate actively in building the Union, contributing to the formulation and implementation of European policies. This participation is exercised through different channels, such as participation in elections, participation in public consultations, submission of petitions, and participation in civil society. Citizens' participation is fundamental to the legitimacy and effectiveness of the Union.

Participation in the formulation of European policies is a fundamental responsibility of citizens. Citizens can influence the formulation of European policies through various channels, such as participation in public consultations, submission of proposals through the European Citizens' Initiative, and participation in citizen deliberation processes. Citizens' participation in the formulation of European policies is fundamental to ensuring that policies reflect citizens' interests and aspirations.

Participation in the implementation of European policies is another responsibility of citizens. Citizens can contribute to the implementation of European policies in various ways, such as participation in volunteering programs, collaboration with local authorities, and promotion of good practices in their communities. Citizens' participation in the implementation of European policies is fundamental to the effectiveness of these policies and to their positive impact on citizens' lives.

Building a stronger, more just, and more solidary Union is the fundamental responsibility of European citizens. Citizens have the responsibility to contribute to building a Union that promotes human dignity, freedom, equality, and solidarity, and that protects the fundamental rights of all citizens. Building a better Union is the shared goal of all European citizens and is fundamental to the future of Europe.

European citizens have the responsibility to transmit European values to future generations, ensuring that the memory of the achievements of the European project is kept alive and that the values of human dignity, freedom, equality, and solidarity are transmitted to new generations. This transmission is carried out through education, culture, historical memory, and participation in democratic life. Transmission of European values is fundamental to the continuity of the European project and to the future of integration.

Education is the main instrument for transmitting European values to future generations. Educational institutions have the responsibility to include in their educational programs learning about the history of European integration, the fundamental values of the Union, and the functioning of European institutions. European education must promote understanding of cultural diversity, respect for human rights, and participation in democratic life. Parents and families also have a fundamental role in transmitting European values to their children, promoting tolerance, solidarity, and commitment to democratic values. European Schools, which are educational institutions established by the Union for the children of European institution staff, are an example of European education that promotes European citizenship values and intercultural understanding.

Culture is another important instrument for transmitting European values. European cultural diversity is one of the main strengths of the European project, and the promotion of cultural exchange among citizens of different member states contributes to building a shared European identity. European cultural programs, such as Erasmus+ and the Creative Europe Programme, promote cultural exchange and mutual understanding among European citizens. Historical memory is also a fundamental instrument for transmitting European values, as the remembrance of past conflicts and the achievements of European integration is essential to building lasting peace. Museums, monuments, and memorial sites play an important role in preserving historical memory and in transmitting European values to future generations. The Union has established the European Heritage Label to recognize initiatives that contribute to the preservation and promotion of European cultural heritage. The promotion of creativity and cultural innovation is also an important part of transmitting European values, as culture is a driver of social change and cohesion. European festivals, exhibitions, and cultural events are an opportunity for citizens of different member states to get to know each other, share experiences, and strengthen bonds of solidarity. The promotion of linguistic diversity is also a fundamental aspect of transmitting European values, as multilingualism is one of the riches of the Union and a condition for mutual understanding among citizens of different member states. Learning foreign languages is an essential skill for European citizens in a globalized world, and the Union promotes language learning through programs such as Erasmus+ and initiatives...

Participation in democratic life is the most direct manifestation of transmitting European values to future generations. Citizens who actively participate in democratic life, voting in elections, participating in public consultations, and contributing to decision-making, are a model for future generations and demonstrate the importance of citizen participation for the functioning of democracy. Participation in democratic life is fundamental to the legitimacy of European institutions and to building a more just and equitable society. Young European citizens have a particularly important role to play in transmitting European values, as they are the heirs of the European project and responsible for its future. The Union has established initiatives to promote young people's participation in democratic life, such as the European Children's Parliament and the Conference on the Future of Europe, which brought together citizens of all ages to debate the future of the Union.
