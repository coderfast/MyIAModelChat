Horizon Europe is the European Union's framework programme for research and innovation for the period 2021-2027, with a budget of 95.5 billion euros, the largest investment in research and innovation ever made by a supranational organisation. This programme succeeds Horizon 2020, which was the previous framework programme for research and innovation, and is designed to address the most urgent global challenges, strengthen European industrial competitiveness and improve the quality of life of European citizens. Horizon Europe is structured around three fundamental pillars: Pillar I, which funds excellent science through the European Research Council (ERC), the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA) for researcher mobility, and European Research Infrastructures; Pillar II, which addresses global challenges and industrial competitiveness through research missions, thematic partnerships and the European Institute of Innovation and Technology (EIT); and Pillar III, which reforms and innovates the European Innovation Space through the European Innovation Council (EIC), SME innovation actions and open innovation. The programme is open to the participation of researchers, innovators, companies, universities, research organisations and public administrations of the Member States of the European Union and associated countries, and promotes international cooperation in research and innovation. Horizon Europe represents a qualitative and quantitative leap compared to its predecessor, with a 30 per cent increase in budget and the introduction of new tools such as research missions, the European Innovation Council and innovation partnerships. The programme is aligned with the political priorities of the European Commission, including the European Green Pact, digital transformation, post-pandemic economic recovery and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. Horizon Europe also introduces new governance mechanisms, such as the Missions Board and the Advisory Groups, which provide strategic guidance on the implementation of the programme.

The strategic objectives of Horizon Europe are organised around three pillars that reflect the political priorities of the European Union in research and innovation. Pillar I aims to strengthen the scientific and technological foundations of the European Union by supporting excellent research, researcher mobility and cutting-edge research infrastructures. This pillar seeks to increase the competitiveness of European research, attract and retain the best scientific talents from around the world, and ensure that Europe remains at the forefront of scientific and technological knowledge. Pillar II aims to address global challenges and strengthen European industrial competitiveness by directing research and innovation towards strategic areas such as health, clean energy, food security, sustainable mobility, climate change and digital transformation. This pillar seeks to generate socioeconomic impact through innovation, improve the quality of life of European citizens and strengthen Europe's position as a global leader in strategic areas. Pillar III aims to reform and innovate the European Innovation Space by supporting innovative companies, especially SMEs, and promoting open innovation and knowledge transfer between the academic world and the private sector. This pillar seeks to close the gap between research and innovation, accelerate the commercialisation of research results and create a more dynamic and connected innovation ecosystem across the European Union. The strategic objectives of Horizon Europe are interconnected and complementary, and their achievement requires cooperation between all actors in the European innovation ecosystem.

The European Research Council (ERC) is the most important component of Pillar I of Horizon Europe and one of the most prestigious research funding institutions in the world. The ERC was established in 2007 as part of the European Union's Framework Programme for Research and Technological Development (FPRTD), and since then has funded more than 12,000 frontier research projects, with a cumulative budget of over 25 billion euros. The ERC is distinguished by its focus on pure scientific excellence, without thematic policy considerations or immediate socioeconomic impact, which allows it to fund frontier research that may not have practical short-term applications but has the potential to generate revolutionary discoveries in the long term. The ERC offers multiple funding programmes, including Consolidator Grants for researchers who are consolidating their careers, Starting Grants for researchers who are beginning their independent careers, Advanced Grants for researchers with established track records, Synergy Grants for teams of researchers collaborating on ambitious projects, and Proof of Concept Grants for researchers seeking to demonstrate the commercial potential of their discoveries. ERC-funded projects are evaluated solely on the basis of scientific excellence, through a high-quality peer review process that involves leading international researchers. The ERC has generated significant impacts on European science, including publications in high-impact journals, patents, spin-offs and contributions to public policy. The ERC has also contributed to attracting scientific talent to Europe, as many leading international researchers have been attracted to Europe by ERC funding opportunities. In Horizon Europe, the ERC has a budget of 16 billion euros, a significant increase that reflects the European Union's commitment to excellence in research.
