# The European Council

## Chapter 1: Composition and Functioning

### The members of the European Council

The European Council is the institution that defines the general political guidelines and strategic priorities of the European Union. It is composed of the heads of state or government of the 27 member states, the President of the European Council and the President of the European Commission. The High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy participates in meetings when foreign policy and security issues are discussed. Unlike the Council of the EU, where representatives may be different ministers depending on the formation, the European Council brings together the top leaders of the countries, which gives it first-rate political authority.

The European Council does not have legislative powers, but its influence on EU policy is enormous, since it defines the broad strategic lines that guide the work of the other institutions. The decisions of the European Council are adopted by consensus, which means that all heads of state or government must agree for a decision to be approved. This consensus requirement reflects respect for the sovereignty of the member states and ensures that all decisions have the unanimous support of national leaders. However, the treaties allow the European Council to adopt decisions by qualified majority in exceptional cases, such as the adoption of measures to deal with a crisis.

The European Council meets at least four times a year, at summits held in Brussels, in the Europa building. These summits are high-level political events where European leaders debate the most important matters affecting the EU. Summits usually last one day, but can extend to two days if negotiations are particularly complex. The European Council summits are preceded by preparatory meetings between the permanent representatives of the member states, who negotiate the texts and seek agreements on the agenda items. Summits are large-scale media events, as the decisions of European leaders are closely followed by the media and citizens.

### The role of the President of the European Council

The President of the European Council is elected by the heads of state or government by qualified majority for a term of two and a half years, renewable once. The President chairs the meetings of the European Council, facilitates cohesion and consensus among the members, and represents the institution externally. The President does not have the power to impose decisions on Council members, but their role as mediator and facilitator is crucial for the functioning of the institution. The President also coordinates the work of the European Council with the other European institutions, especially the European Commission and the European Parliament.

The President of the European Council has several important functions. First, they prepare and chair the summits, proposing the agenda and facilitating negotiations among national leaders. Second, the President seeks consensus among Council members and may propose compromises to overcome differences. Third, the President represents the European Council in relations with the other European institutions and with world leaders. Fourth, the President coordinates the work of the European Council with the Council of the EU, ensuring coherence between the strategic decisions of the European Council and the legislative decisions of the Council. Fifth, the President informs the European Parliament about the activities of the European Council, keeping the Parliament informed of strategic decisions.

The first permanent President of the European Council was Herman Van Rompuy (2009-2014), who played an important role in managing the euro crisis and consolidating the new institution. Donald Tusk (2014-2019) faced the challenges of the migration crisis, Brexit and Russian aggression against Ukraine. Charles Michel (2019-2024) presided over the Council during the COVID-19 pandemic and the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and his term was characterized by crisis management and the search for collective responses. Antonio Costa (2024-present) is the current President of the European Council, and his term focuses on competitiveness, defense and EU enlargement.

## Chapter 2: European Council Meetings

### European summits

European summits are the most important meetings of the European Council, where heads of state or government debate and adopt decisions on the most urgent and strategic matters of the EU. Summits are held at least four times a year, but can be convened more frequently if the situation requires it. During summits, European leaders debate topics such as the economy, security, foreign policy, climate change, migration and the future of European integration. Summits are high-level political events where leaders take decisions that can have a significant impact on the lives of European citizens.

European summits follow a strict protocol, which includes the arrival of leaders at the Europa building, official photographs, restricted-format meetings (only leaders and their closest advisers) and extended-format meetings (with the participation of commissioners and other representatives). Summits usually begin with a dinner or working lunch, where leaders debate informally about agenda items. Negotiations at summits can be intense, as leaders defend the interests of their countries and seek compromises that satisfy everyone. Summits end with the adoption of European Council conclusions, which establish the decisions and political guidelines agreed upon.

European summits also include specific meetings, such as eurozone summits, security summits and foreign relations summits. Eurozone summits bring together the leaders of eurozone countries to debate monetary policy, financial stability and economic governance. Security summits bring together leaders to debate defense, terrorism and cybersecurity. Foreign relations summits bring together leaders to debate relations with third countries and international organizations. These thematic summits allow for a more detailed examination of specific issues and facilitate decision-making in complex areas.

### Informal meetings

In addition to official summits, the European Council holds informal meetings, known as mini-summits or informal summits, which allow European leaders to debate strategic issues in a more relaxed manner. These informal meetings are usually held in less formal settings than the Europa building, such as castles or official residences, and allow for more open and direct dialogue among leaders. Informal meetings are useful for preparing official summits, for discussing sensitive issues that require confidential dialogue, or for seeking solutions to problems that are stalled in official meetings.

Informal meetings of the European Council may also include working meals or walks, which create a conducive environment for informal dialogue. European leaders take advantage of these occasions to get to know each other better, understand the concerns of their counterparts and seek creative solutions to pending problems. Informal meetings do not produce official conclusions, but can be decisive for the preparation of official summits and for the search for compromises. The ability of leaders to debate informally is a strength of the European Council, as it allows overcoming differences that cannot be resolved in official meetings.

Informal meetings are also important for strengthening cohesion among European leaders. Personal trust among leaders is a crucial factor for the search for consensus, and informal meetings provide opportunities to build and maintain that trust. European leaders take advantage of informal meetings to discuss not only official matters, but also more general questions about the future of Europe and relations between their countries. These informal discussions can be as important as official decisions, as they create a common understanding that facilitates the search for solutions in official meetings.

## Chapter 3: Strategic Decisions

### General political guidelines

The European Council has the responsibility of defining the general political guidelines and strategic priorities of the European Union. These guidelines guide the work of the other European institutions, especially the European Commission, which translates them into concrete legislative proposals and policies. The guidelines of the European Council cover all areas of EU policy, from the economy and defense to education and culture. The decisions of the European Council are adopted by consensus, which ensures that all guidelines have the unanimous support of national leaders.

The European Council defines the strategic priorities of the EU through various instruments. First, the European Council conclusions establish the decisions and political guidelines adopted at each summit. Second, the European Council's strategic agenda defines political priorities for a five-year period and serves as a guide for the work of the Commission and Parliament. Third, joint declarations and press releases of the European Council communicate the positions of European leaders on current issues. These instruments are essential to ensure the coherence of European policy and to guide the work of the institutions.

The guidelines of the European Council have a significant impact on EU policy. For example, the European Council's decisions on the euro crisis led to the creation of the European Stability Mechanism and the adoption of new fiscal rules. Decisions on the refugee crisis led to the creation of relocation mechanisms and the expansion of external border operations. Decisions on Brexit led to the withdrawal negotiations and negotiations on future relations. Decisions on the COVID-19 pandemic led to the creation of the Next Generation EU recovery fund and the adoption of vaccination strategies. These examples show how the strategic decisions of the European Council have a concrete impact on the lives of European citizens.

### Common Foreign and Security Policy

The European Council plays a fundamental role in the Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) of the European Union. The Council defines the guidelines of the CFSP and adopts the necessary decisions for its implementation. CFSP decisions include the adoption of common positions, joint actions and restrictive measures (sanctions) against countries or entities that pose a threat to international peace and security. The European Council also defines EU strategies on defense, security and foreign relations, and monitors their implementation.

The CFSP is an area where national sovereignty is particularly important, as decisions on foreign policy and security directly affect the defense and national interests of the member states. For this reason, CFSP decisions are taken by consensus, and member states retain the principal responsibility for their foreign and defense policy. However, the EU has developed significant capabilities in areas such as peace missions, crisis management, counter-terrorism and cybersecurity, and has created instruments to finance these activities. The European Council oversees the coherence between the CFSP and other EU policies, and ensures that the EU's external actions are aligned with its values and objectives.

The European Council also plays an important role in the EU's relations with international organizations and partner countries. European leaders regularly participate in international summits, such as the G7, G20 and bilateral summits with other regional blocs, and the European Council defines the EU's positions at these summits. The European Council also adopts decisions on EU enlargement, partnership with neighboring countries and cooperation with international organizations. These decisions are fundamental to the EU's position in the world and its relations with international partners.

## Chapter 4: The Presidency of the European Council

### The role of the President

The President of the European Council is a key figure in the governance of the European Union, as they chair the Council meetings and represent the institution externally. The President is elected by the heads of state or government by qualified majority, and their term lasts two and a half years, renewable once. The President does not have the power to impose decisions on Council members, but their role as mediator and facilitator is crucial for the functioning of the institution. The President must be a person with political authority and negotiating ability, capable of building consensus among leaders with different interests.

The functions of the President of the European Council are varied and demanding. First, the President prepares and chairs the summits, proposing the agenda and facilitating negotiations. Second, the President seeks consensus among Council members and may propose compromises to overcome differences. Third, the President represents the European Council in relations with the other European institutions, especially the European Commission and the European Parliament. Fourth, the President represents the EU externally, participating in international summits and maintaining relations with world leaders. Fifth, the President informs the European Parliament about the activities of the European Council, keeping the Parliament informed of strategic decisions.

The President of the European Council also plays an important role in crisis management. When emergencies arise, such as the COVID-19 pandemic or the Russian invasion of Ukraine, the President convenes extraordinary meetings of the European Council and coordinates the responses of national leaders. The President may also convene ad hoc summits to address urgent issues, and may maintain direct contacts with world leaders to seek solutions to international problems. The President's ability to manage crises is essential for the credibility and effectiveness of the European Council.

## Chapter 5: The European Council and the Council of the EU

### Differences between the two institutions

The European Council and the Council of the European Union are two different institutions, although they share the same name and meet in the same building. The fundamental difference is that the European Council brings together heads of state or government, while the Council of the EU brings together ministers from different policy areas. The European Council defines the general political guidelines and strategic priorities, while the Council of the EU adopts legislative and implementation decisions in cooperation with the European Parliament. These differences are fundamental to understanding the EU's institutional system.

The European Council does not have legislative powers, but its influence on EU policy is enormous, as its strategic decisions guide the work of the other institutions. The Council of the EU, for its part, has significant legislative and implementation powers, as it adopts the majority of European laws in cooperation with the Parliament. The Council of the EU also manages the EU budget together with the Parliament, and coordinates the economic policies of the member states. The differences between the European Council and the Council of the EU are essential for the balance of power in the EU and for the separation between strategic and legislative functions.

The relationship between the European Council and the Council of the EU is complex and has been the subject of debate. Some argue that the European Council should have legislative powers to ensure that strategic decisions are translated into concrete actions. Others argue that the European Council should limit itself to defining general guidelines, leaving legislative decisions to the Council of the EU and the Parliament. The Treaty of Lisbon attempted to clarify the relations between the two institutions, clearly establishing the functions and powers of each. However, in practice, the relations between the European Council and the Council of the EU remain subject to interpretation and political negotiation.

### Cooperation between the two institutions

Despite their differences, the European Council and the Council of the EU cooperate closely in the decision-making process. The European Council defines the strategic guidelines that guide the work of the Council of the EU, and the Council of the EU implements these guidelines through legislative and implementation decisions. This cooperation is essential to ensure the coherence of European policy, as the strategic decisions of the European Council must be translated into concrete actions by the Council of the EU. Cooperation is facilitated by the fact that both Councils meet in the same building and share the same secretariat.

Cooperation between the European Council and the Council of the EU is manifested in various areas. In the economic area, the European Council defines the guidelines of economic policy, and the Council of the EU adopts the necessary regulations and directives for their implementation. In the defense area, the European Council defines security strategies, and the Council of the EU coordinates defense actions and peace missions. In the area of justice and home affairs, the European Council defines priorities on migration and security, and the Council of the EU adopts the necessary legislation. This division of labor is essential for the effectiveness of European governance.

Cooperation between the European Council and the Council of the EU is also facilitated by the rotating presidency of the Council of the EU, which changes every six months among the member states. The member state holding the presidency of the Council of the EU coordinates the work of the Council and represents the institution before the other European institutions. The President of the European Council, for their part, coordinates the work of the European Council and represents the institution externally. Cooperation between the two presidents is essential to ensure coherence between the strategic decisions of the European Council and the legislative decisions of the Council of the EU.

## Chapter 6: The Informal Dimension

### Personal diplomacy

The European Council is an institution where personal diplomacy plays a crucial role. Informal meetings, working dinners and bilateral encounters between European leaders are opportunities for direct dialogue and the search for compromises. Personal trust among leaders is a determining factor in the European Council's ability to take quick and effective decisions. European leaders take advantage of summits to maintain personal contacts, understand the concerns of their counterparts and seek creative solutions to pending problems.

Personal diplomacy at the European Council manifests itself in various ways. Leaders often hold bilateral meetings before or after summits to discuss specific issues affecting their countries. Working meals are an opportunity for informal dialogue, where leaders can express their opinions more openly than in official meetings. Leaders also maintain telephone and videoconference contacts between summits to follow up on adopted decisions and to discuss urgent matters. The ability of leaders to communicate directly and personally is a strength of the European Council, as it allows overcoming differences that cannot be resolved in official meetings.

Personal diplomacy at the European Council also has limitations. European leaders have different political agendas and often have to balance European interests with national interests. The pressure of public opinion and national parliaments can hinder the search for compromises, as leaders may be criticized for making concessions to other countries. Personal diplomacy also depends on personal relationships between leaders, which can change with national elections and political changes. Despite these limitations, personal diplomacy remains an essential element of the functioning of the European Council.

### The role of permanent representatives

The permanent representatives of the member states in the EU are senior diplomats who represent their governments before the European institutions. The permanent representatives meet in the Permanent Representatives Committee (COREPER), which prepares the meetings of the Council of the EU and the European Council. COREPER meets weekly in Brussels, and its meetings are preceded by working meetings where national experts examine the technical details of proposals. The permanent representatives are assisted by teams of experts who provide advice on specific policy areas.

Permanent representatives play a crucial role in the preparation of European Council decisions. Before each summit, permanent representatives meet to negotiate the texts of the European Council conclusions and seek agreements on agenda items. These negotiations can be complex and prolonged, as permanent representatives defend the interests of their countries and seek compromises that satisfy everyone. Permanent representatives also coordinate the position of their governments before the other European institutions, especially before the European Commission and the European Parliament.

Permanent representatives are assisted by deputy permanent representatives and by counselors of the permanent representations, who handle specific policy areas. These officials meet in specialized committees, which examine the technical details of proposals and prepare COREPER meetings. The network of officials from the permanent representations is essential for the preparation of European Council decisions, as it provides the technical and political information necessary for decision-making. Permanent representatives and their teams are, in the final analysis, those who do the preparatory work that enables European leaders to make informed and effective decisions at summits.

## Chapter 7: The European Council and Foreign Policy

### External representation

The European Council plays a fundamental role in the external representation of the European Union. The President of the European Council represents the EU in relations with world leaders and participates in international summits, such as the G7, G20 and bilateral summits with other regional blocs. The President of the European Council also coordinates the EU's positions in international organizations, such as the United Nations, NATO and the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE). The European Council's external representation is essential to ensure that the EU acts as a single actor on the international stage.

The European Council also adopts decisions on the common foreign and security policy (CFSP), which is the framework for the EU's external action. The Council defines the guidelines of the CFSP and adopts the necessary decisions for its implementation, including the adoption of common positions, joint actions and restrictive measures (sanctions). The European Council also defines EU strategies on defense, security and foreign relations, and monitors their implementation. The CFSP is an area where national sovereignty remains important, as decisions are taken by consensus and member states retain the principal responsibility for their foreign and defense policy.

The external representation of the European Council also has a symbolic dimension. European summits are large-scale media events, and the decisions of European leaders are closely followed by the media and citizens around the world. The statements and actions of the European Council are interpreted as the EU's position on international issues, and have an impact on Europe's image abroad. The European Council's ability to speak with a single voice is essential for the EU's credibility as an international actor, and for the defense of its values and interests abroad.

### Cooperation with other international organizations

The European Council maintains close relations with international organizations, such as the United Nations, NATO, the OSCE and the African Union. The European Council coordinates the EU's position in these organizations and participates in their activities and decisions. Cooperation with the United Nations is particularly important, as the EU and its member states are responsible for approximately 30% of contributions to the UN budget and represent a significant share of peacekeeping operations. The European Council also supports the reform of the UN Security Council to make it more representative and effective.

Cooperation with NATO is another key area for the European Council. The majority of EU member states are also NATO members, which creates significant overlap in the areas of defense and security. The European Council coordinates the EU's position in NATO and oversees cooperation between the two organizations in areas such as cybersecurity, counter-terrorism and crisis management. The relationship between the EU and NATO is complex, as some areas of cooperation fall under EU competence, while others fall under NATO competence. The European Council has the responsibility of ensuring that cooperation between the EU and NATO is effective and avoids duplication.

Cooperation with the OSCE is important for stability and security in Europe. The OSCE is an organization that encompasses 57 states from Europe, Asia and North America, and deals with issues such as security, human rights and democracy. The European Council coordinates the EU's position in the OSCE and supports its activities in areas such as conflict prevention, election observation missions and the protection of human rights. Cooperation with the OSCE is essential for European stability, as it allows addressing security issues that affect the EU and its neighbors.

## Chapter 8: The European Council and Economic Governance

### Supervision of economic policy

The European Council plays an important role in supervising the economic policy of the European Union. The Council defines the guidelines of economic policy and coordinates the actions of member states to ensure stability and economic growth. The European Council also adopts decisions on the economic governance of the eurozone, including the adoption of the Stability and Growth Pact and the creation of financial stability mechanisms. Supervision of economic policy is a priority for the European Council, as economic stability is essential for the well-being of citizens and for the cohesion of the EU.

The Stability and Growth Pact is a set of rules designed to ensure that member states maintain sound public finances. The pact sets limits for public deficit (3% of GDP) and public debt (60% of GDP), and provides for sanctions against countries that do not comply with these rules. The European Council oversees the application of the pact and adopts decisions on sanctions against non-compliant countries. The European Council has also adopted decisions to relax the rules during periods of crisis, such as during the COVID-19 pandemic, when the pact's limitations were suspended to allow governments to spend more to deal with the pandemic.

The European Council also coordinates the economic policies of member states, seeking to ensure the coherence and complementarity of national policies. The Council examines the stability and convergence programs of member states, which describe each country's economic and fiscal strategies, and may make recommendations to improve economic management. The European Council also adopts decisions on the EU's trade policy, competition policy and industrial policy. Supervision of economic policy is essential to ensure that economic integration benefits all member states and all citizens.

### The eurozone

The European Council plays a fundamental role in the governance of the eurozone. The Council defines the guidelines of monetary policy and supervises the financial stability of the eurozone. The European Council also adopts decisions on the enlargement of the eurozone, the adoption of the euro by new member states and the reform of eurozone institutions. The eurozone is one of the most important areas of economic integration in the EU, and its stability is essential for the economy of the entire continent.

The European Council coordinates the economic policy of eurozone countries, seeking to ensure the convergence of economies and price stability. The Council examines the reports of the European Stability Mechanism and the European Financial Stability Facility, and adopts decisions on financial assistance to countries in difficulty. The European Council also coordinates the exchange rate policy of the eurozone and supervises relations with the European Central Bank. Eurozone governance is a complex area, as it requires coordination among national governments, the European Commission and the ECB.

The European Council has also adopted decisions to strengthen eurozone governance, including the creation of the European Stability Mechanism, the adoption of the reinforced Stability and Growth Pact and the creation of the Single Supervisory Mechanism. These reforms seek to improve the resilience of the eurozone and prevent future crises. The European Council has also discussed the possibility of creating a common eurozone budget and a European finance ministry, but these proposals have encountered opposition from some member states. Eurozone governance remains a subject of debate and negotiation in the European Council.

The history of the European Council dates back to the early years of European integration, when the founders of the ECSC sought to create institutions that could manage the common policies of the member states. The institution was created with a clear mandate to serve the interests of the European Union and its citizens, and has since played a fundamental role in the development of European integration. Over the decades, the institution has evolved to adapt to the political, economic and social changes of the continent, and has expanded its competences to respond to new challenges.

The institution had to develop its own rules and procedures, and had to create an administration capable of managing the increasingly complex policies of the European Union. The institution also had to establish relations with the other European institutions and with national governments, which required the creation of cooperation and coordination mechanisms.

Each treaty has represented a significant advance in European integration and has allowed the institution to play a broader and more effective role in European policy.

The Treaty of Lisbon of 2009 was particularly important for the institution, as it significantly expanded its competences in areas such as foreign policy, defense and judicial cooperation. The treaty also established new decision-making procedures and strengthened the democratic nature of European institutions by expanding the powers of the European Parliament. The institution had to adapt to these changes and had to develop new strategies to address the new competences and new procedures.

The institutional evolution of the institution has also been influenced by changes in the political and economic context of Europe. The enlargement of the European Union to the countries of Eastern Europe, the euro crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic have been some of the challenges that the institution has had to face, and the institution has demonstrated a remarkable capacity for adaptation in response to these challenges. The evolution of the institution is a testament to the importance of flexibility and adaptability for the effective functioning of European institutions.
