Cereals and tubers form the base of the food pyramid in many cultures and are the primary source of energy for most of the world's population. Vitamins are essential organic compounds that the body needs in small quantities for the proper functioning of multiple physiological processes. Unlike macronutrients (carbohydrates, protein, and fats), vitamins do not provide energy directly, but are necessary for releasing energy from food and for regulating numerous bodily functions. Vitamins are classified into two main groups: fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, and K), which are stored in the body's fatty tissue, and water-soluble vitamins (B vitamins and vitamin C), which are not significantly stored and must be consumed regularly. Vitamin deficiency can cause serious health problems, from blindness (vitamin A deficiency) to scurvy (vitamin C deficiency).
