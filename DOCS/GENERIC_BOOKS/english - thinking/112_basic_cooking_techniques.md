Understanding heat and how it affects food is perhaps the most critical knowledge a cook can possess, as the difference between a perfectly cooked dish and a ruined one often comes down to a matter of degrees and seconds. Heat transfer occurs through three main mechanisms in cooking: conduction, where heat passes directly from a hot surface to food in contact with it; convection, where heat moves through a liquid or gas medium such as water, oil, or air; and radiation, where heat travels through electromagnetic waves as in grilling or broiling. Each mechanism has its own characteristics and applications, and understanding them allows the cook to choose the most appropriate method for each ingredient and desired result.
