The Erasmus program is one of the most celebrated successes of the European Union and one of the largest and most recognized educational exchange programs in the world. Its history began in 1987, when the European Commission officially launched the Community Action Programme for the Mobility of University Students, known as Erasmus, with the objective of promoting cooperation between European universities and facilitating the academic mobility of students. The name Erasmus is an acronym for European Region Action Scheme for the Mobility of University Students, and also pays homage to the Dutch humanist and philosopher of the 15th century Desiderius Erasmus of Rotterdam, known simply as Erasmus, who was a defender of learning and intellectual cooperation across borders. The idea of a European university exchange program emerged in the 1980s, driven by growing European integration and the need to foster mutual understanding and European identity among younger generations. The first framework agreement for university mobility was adopted by the Council of Ministers of Education of the European Economic Community in June 1987, and the program entered into force in the 1987-1988 academic year, with the initial participation of 3,244 students from 11 participating countries. From its humble beginnings, the Erasmus program has grown exponentially to become the largest educational exchange program in the world, having enabled more than 14 million people to participate in educational mobility activities since 1987. The program has had a profound impact on European education, European identity and social cohesion, and is considered one of the most successful and popular programs of the European Union, with citizen support consistently exceeding 80 percent in European public opinion polls.

The Erasmus program has undergone significant evolution since its creation in 1987, adapting to changes in the European educational landscape and progressively expanding its scope, objectives and target groups. In its first phase (1987-1994), the program focused exclusively on the mobility of university students, providing scholarships for study stays at universities in other European countries. In 1994, the program was expanded to include the mobility of university professors, enabling professors to teach at universities in other European countries and learn from educational experiences in other national contexts. In 1995, the program was integrated into the first EU education and training framework programme, Socrates, which brought together several European educational programs under one umbrella. In 1999, with the adoption of the Bologna Process, which aimed to create a compatible and comparable European Higher Education Area, the Erasmus program acquired a new dimension by becoming a fundamental tool for the mobility of students and professors within the European Higher Education Area. In 2007, the Erasmus program was integrated into the Lifelong Learning programme, which extended its reach to higher education, vocational training, school education and adult education. In 2014, the Erasmus program was integrated into Erasmus+, which combines all EU education, training, youth and sport programs into a single program, with a budget of 14.7 billion euros for the 2014-2020 period.
