### Stromatolites and Early Life

Stromatolites are laminated sedimentary structures formed by the activity of microbial communities, primarily cyanobacteria, that trap and cement sedimentary particles creating dome-shaped or mat-like structures that can reach several meters in diameter and that represent some of the oldest evidence of life on Earth. The oldest known stromatolites come from the Dresser and Strelley Pool formations in Pilbara, Western Australia, with an age of 3.480-3.430 billion years, and show microscopic laminated structures consistent with the activity of photosynthetic microbial communities. The stromatolites of the Fig Tree formation in South Africa, from 3.220 billion years ago, contain microscopic fossil filaments that are interpreted as the remains of primitive cyanobacteria, although some scientists argue they could be abiotic structures. The stromatolites of the Bishop formation in Western Australia, from 3.430 billion years ago, contain microstructures that resemble modern cyanobacteria of the genus Lyngbya, suggesting that oxygenic photosynthesis already existed 3.430 billion years ago. Modern stromatolites are found in extreme environments such as the lagoons of Shark Bay in Western Australia, the hot springs of Yellowstone in the United States, and the salt ponds of Spain, where cyanobacteria form structures similar to fossils, providing a model for interpreting ancient stromatolites. Stromatolites are not only evidence of early life but also records of past environmental conditions, since the isotopic composition of carbon in fossil stromatolites indicates photosynthetic activity, the presence of oxygen, and the temperature of the water in which they formed.

### Trilobites

Trilobites were a group of marine arthropods that dominated the oceans during the Cambrian Period and persisted until the Permian extinction, 252 million years ago, spanning more than 220 million years of evolutionary history, making them one of the most successful animal groups in Earth's history. ## Chapter 15: The Ordovician Period

### Marine Diversification and the First Extinction

The Ordovician Period spans from approximately 485 million years ago to 444 million years ago, and was characterized by an explosive diversification of marine life, the appearance of the first vertebrates, the colonization of land by plants, and one of the five great extinctions in history, the Ordovician-Silurian extinction, which eliminated 85% of marine species.
