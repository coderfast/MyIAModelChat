Sustainable and ethical clothing stores, increasingly numerous, offer garments made with organic materials, environmentally friendly processes, and fair labor conditions. Sports clothing stores, like Nike, Adidas, or Decathlon, specialize in sports equipment and technical clothing that combines functionality and style. ### 1.2 Planning your clothing shopping

Before going clothes shopping, careful planning helps us maximize the efficiency of our search, avoid impulse purchases, and ensure that the garments we acquire integrate harmoniously into our existing wardrobe. The first step of planning is to review our current wardrobe to identify what garments we have, which ones need to be replaced, and what pieces are missing to complete versatile and functional outfits. Creating a specific shopping list, organized by categories (t-shirts, pants, dresses, coats) and priorities (essential, desired, optional), provides us with a clear map that guides our search and avoids costly detours. Establishing a realistic budget before going shopping is fundamental to keeping within our financial limits and avoiding guilt or economic stress afterward. Researching current fashion trends helps us identify garments that will be in style for more than one season, maximizing the value of our investment. Checking the return policies of the stores we plan to visit gives us peace of mind and flexibility in case a garment doesn't fit perfectly or doesn't convince us when trying it on at home. Planning the day and time of shopping avoids peak hours when stores are fullest, fitting rooms have wait times, and staff are most overwhelmed. Considering seasonal weather and the specific occasions for which we need clothing (work, formal events, sports, travel) helps us prioritize the most urgent purchases. Planning clothing shopping is not a restriction on spontaneity but a tool that allows us to enjoy the shopping experience with greater awareness, satisfaction, and financial responsibility.

### 1.3 The selection process

The process of selecting garments in a clothing store involves a combination of aesthetic, practical, and personal factors that we must balance to make satisfying short- and long-term decisions. Size is the most basic and fundamental factor: a garment that doesn't fit well, no matter how beautiful, will not be used and will represent a waste of money and resources. Different sizing systems between brands and countries can create confusion, so it is advisable to try garments on in the fitting room before deciding, regardless of the size indicated on the label. Material quality is another crucial aspect: natural fabrics like cotton, wool, silk, and linen tend to be more comfortable, durable, and environmentally friendly than synthetics like polyester or nylon. Garment construction is evaluated by examining seams, buttons, zippers, and finishes: well-made garments will last longer and maintain their appearance after multiple washes. Versatility is a valuable criterion: choosing garments that can be easily combined with others already in our wardrobe maximizes the number of possible outfits with a limited number of pieces. Color should be considered in relation to our skin tone, hair color, and the predominant colors in our current wardrobe. Personal style, although it may evolve over time, should be coherent with the image we want to project and our lifestyle. Garment comfort is a factor often underestimated in the store but becomes determining during daily use. Price, although important, should be evaluated in relation to the quality, durability, and expected frequency of use of the garment, not simply as the sole deciding factor.

### 1.4 The fitting rooms

Fitting rooms are one of the most important spaces in a clothing store, as they allow us to verify how a garment actually fits our body, something that is impossible to determine just by looking at it on the hanger. The fitting room experience begins with choosing the garments we want to try, ideally a manageable number (between three and five garments) that allows us to compare options without being overwhelmed. When entering the fitting room, it is important to verify that the lighting is adequate for evaluating colors and textures, and that there is a full-length mirror that allows viewing the complete garment. The process of trying on a garment includes observing how the fabric falls over our body, verifying that the seams are well aligned, checking mobility (sitting down, raising arms, walking), and evaluating the comfort of the size. It is advisable to try on garments with the underwear and accessories we would normally wear with them, as this significantly affects the fit and final appearance. If we are considering combining the new garment with other existing pieces in our wardrobe, it is helpful to bring a sample of the color or fabric we want to match to verify compatibility. Fitting rooms are also a space for reflection: if we are hesitant about a garment, it is better to leave it and return later than to make an impulsive decision under the pressure of the moment. When leaving the fitting room, it is courteous to leave the space tidy for the next user, hanging the garments we don't want or leaving them in the designated area. If none of the tried garments convinces us, it is completely valid to leave without buying anything, a decision that saves money and prevents the accumulation of unnecessary clothing in our wardrobe.

### 1.5 Responsible shopping

Responsible clothing shopping involves considering not only our personal needs and desires but also the environmental, social, and economic impact of our consumption decisions. The fashion industry is one of the most polluting sectors in the world, responsible for approximately ten percent of global carbon emissions and the second-largest consumer of water after agriculture. Responsible shopping begins with reduction: buying less but better, investing in quality garments that last longer and adapt to different seasons and occasions. Choosing garments made from sustainable materials like organic cotton, responsibly raised sheep wool, bamboo fibers, or recycled materials significantly reduces our environmental impact. Supporting brands and stores that have ethical, transparent production practices and respect the labor rights of their workers is a form of voting with our money that promotes a fairer industry. Buying second-hand, through charity shops, online platforms like Vinted or Wallapop, or clothing markets, is one of the most effective ways to reduce textile waste and find unique pieces at reduced prices. Proper care of the clothing we already own, including washing at lower temperatures, repairing damage, and proper storage, extends the lifespan of garments and delays the need to replace them. Impulse purchases, motivated by discounts, fleeting trends, or emotional states, are the main cause of unused clothing ending up in landfills. Developing a defined personal style helps us resist the pressure of ephemeral trends and build a capsule wardrobe with versatile pieces that combine with each other and remain relevant across multiple seasons. ## Chapter 2: In the Supermarket

### 2.1 Supermarket organization

Supermarkets are carefully designed using principles of consumer psychology and traffic management to influence our shopping patterns and maximize sales. The typical layout of a supermarket begins with fresh fruits and vegetables at the entrance, creating a first impression of freshness and quality that positively predisposes the buyer. Bakery and pastry products are usually placed near the entrance for their attractive aroma that stimulates appetite and the desire to purchase. Basic and daily consumption products like milk, eggs, and bread are generally placed at the back or in the farthest corners from the entrance, forcing the buyer to walk through the entire store and be exposed to more products. Aisles for snacks, drinks, and seasonal products are usually strategically placed to capture the buyer's attention while walking through the store toward essential products. Checkout areas are designed to create a last opportunity for purchase with small-format, affordable products that can be added to the cart on impulse. Signage and lighting are key tools of supermarket design: clear signs and adequate lighting facilitate navigation and product location, while special lighting highlights promotions and offers. The most modern supermarkets incorporate technology such as QR codes for product information, mobile apps with personalized coupons, and contactless payment systems that streamline the shopping experience. Background music, store temperature, and even wall colors are elements designed to create an atmosphere that encourages staying and purchasing. Knowing these supermarket design tricks allows us to be more conscious and informed shoppers, resisting environmental influences and making decisions based on our real needs.

### 2.2 Choosing fresh products

The selection of fresh products, such as fruits, vegetables, meat, and fish, is one of the most important and challenging tasks of supermarket shopping, since the quality of these ingredients largely determines the outcome of our culinary preparations. For fruits, ripeness indicators include color (each fruit has its optimal color spectrum), texture (firm but slightly yielding when gently pressed), aroma (ripe fruits have an intense and pleasant smell), and weight (heavier fruits for their size tend to be juicier). Fresh vegetables are identified by their turgid, brightly colored leaves, firm stems, absence of spots or wilting, and a clean, fresh smell. Fresh meat should have a uniform, bright color, with creamy white fat (not yellowish), firm texture to the touch, and a neutral or slightly metallic smell, never sour or unpleasant. Fresh fish is recognized by its bright, adhered scales, transparent and bulging eyes, bright red gills, firm flesh that recovers its shape when pressed, and a clean sea smell, never ammonia. Fresh seafood should have intact shells, firm flesh, and a fresh marine smell. Fresh dairy products are checked by their expiration dates, container integrity, and absence of bulging or discoloration. Fresh breads are identified by their crispy crust, spongy crumb, and toasted wheat aroma. Choosing fresh products requires practice and attention, but developing these skills significantly improves the quality of our food and the satisfaction of our culinary preparations.

### 2.3 Reading labels

Reading food labels is a fundamental skill for modern consumers that allows making informed decisions about what we buy and consume, considering aspects such as nutritional quality, allergens, ingredient composition, and storage conditions. The ingredient list, which must appear in descending order of weight, tells us exactly what the product contains and in what proportion: if the first ingredient is sugar, we know the product is mainly sugar. Nutritional values, generally presented in a standardized table, provide information about calories, fats (saturated and unsaturated), carbohydrates (sugars), proteins, fiber, sodium, and other nutrients per serving of the product. Allergen warning labels, mandatory in many countries, identify the presence of the most common allergens such as gluten, milk, eggs, nuts, soy, and fish. Expiration dates and best-before dates are different but equally important indicators: expiration indicates that the product should not be consumed after that date, while best-before indicates that the product maintains all its properties until that date but can be consumed afterward with some loss of quality. Quality certifications, such as organic, fair trade, protected designation of origin, or animal welfare certifications, provide information about the product's production standards. Food additives, identified by E numbers in the European Union, are substances added to improve preservation, texture, color, or flavor of the product, and their presence may be an indicator of lower product naturalness. ### 2.4 Shopping cart management

The shopping cart, or basket, is our main tool during the supermarket experience, and its effective management influences shopping efficiency, budget, and satisfaction with the products purchased. Starting shopping with a clean cart in good condition facilitates the experience and avoids problems like defective wheels or dirty spaces. Organizing the cart during shopping is important: fragile products like eggs, bread, or soft fruits should be placed separately from heavier ones to prevent crushing. Frozen products should be grouped together to minimize their exposure to room temperature and maintain their state for as long as possible. Liquid products like milk, juices, or oils should be placed stably to prevent spills that could damage other products. Keeping a visible shopping list during the process helps us follow our original plan and avoid impulse purchases outside the list. The distribution of product categories in the cart is also relevant: it is helpful to group by type (fresh with fresh, canned with canned) to facilitate review before paying and organization upon arriving home. If we use reusable bags, it is advisable to carry them in the cart from the start to remember them when reaching the checkout. The weight of the cart should be manageable: a cart that is too full can be difficult to maneuver and may cause us to overlook products we need. Reviewing the cart before approaching the checkout allows us to verify that we haven't forgotten anything essential and that we haven't included products we don't really need. Conscious shopping cart management is a practical skill that improves with experience and transforms a potentially stressful task into an efficient and pleasant activity.

### 2.5 Online supermarket shopping

Online supermarket shopping has experienced exponential growth in recent years, driven by the convenience they offer, mobile technology, and more recently, changes in consumption habits resulting from the pandemic. Online supermarket platforms like Mercadona a Domicilio, Carrefour, Amazon Fresh, or Glovo Market allow completing the full shopping trip from home, with the ability to filter products by categories, brands, prices, and promotions. The main advantage of online supermarket shopping is comfort: there is no need to travel, no crowds, and shopping arrives at our door at the time we choose. Price comparison is easier in the online format, where we can quickly verify the price per unit of measure, available offers, and the most economical alternatives. Supermarket apps usually offer discount coupons, loyalty programs, and personalized recommendations that can result in significant savings. However, online supermarket shopping also presents challenges: we cannot touch or smell fresh products, the selection may be more limited than in the physical store, and delivery times may not always be the most convenient. Shipping costs and minimum orders are factors to consider: some supermarkets offer free shipping starting from a minimum amount, while others charge a fixed or variable fee. Return management is another aspect to consider: if a product doesn't satisfy us, the return process may be more complex than in a physical store. To optimize online supermarket shopping, it is advisable to create frequently used shopping lists that can be repeated with a click, verify return policies before purchasing, and plan the order reception to ensure someone is at home. ## Chapter 3: At the Traditional Market

### 3.1 The market experience

Traditional markets represent one of the most authentic and enriching commercial experiences that exist, combining the sale of fresh products with a social and cultural dimension that makes them true epicenters of community life. Unlike supermarkets, where human interaction is minimal and shopping is an impersonal transaction, markets offer an atmosphere of closeness where buyer and seller maintain a relationship that transcends the mere commercial transaction. The atmosphere of a traditional market is a symphony of colors, smells, and sounds: fruit and vegetable stalls display their products carefully arranged in mountains of vibrant colors, fishmongers offer their catch of the day with animated shouts, and butchers cut pieces with the precision of experienced artisans. The smells of the market are equally evocative: the aroma of fresh herbs like basil, cilantro, and parsley mixes with the earthy smell of mushrooms, the perfume of ripe fruits, and the salty scent of the sea emanating from the seafood stalls. The social dimension of the market is one of its most cherished values: vendors know their regular customers by name, their preferences, and their habits, creating trust relationships that go beyond the sale. Markets also function as social gathering spaces where neighbors cross paths, converse, and share neighborhood news, strengthening the community fabric in a way that supermarkets can hardly replicate. The tradition of the market, passed down from generation to generation in many cultures, represents an intangible heritage that deserves to be preserved and celebrated. Participating in market life is a way to connect with the cultural roots of a place, learn about the history of the products we consume, and support local producers who keep culinary traditions alive.

Bakery and pastry stalls offer freshly baked breads, traditional pastries, cakes, and confectionery products that fill the market with aromas of butter and cinnamon. Butcher stalls display a variety of fresh meats, artisanal sausages, and prepared products that reflect the culinary traditions of the region. Fishmonger stalls are scenes of frenetic activity, especially in the mornings, with fresh fish of the day that shine with silver scales and transparent eyes. Cheese stalls offer a selection of local, national, and international cheeses, with the possibility of tasting before buying, an experience that supermarkets can hardly replicate. ### 3.5 Sustainability in markets

Traditional markets are, by their very nature, one of the most sustainable commercial models that exist, and their preservation and promotion are fundamental to building food systems that are more respectful of the environment and local communities. Direct sales from producer to consumer, which is the essence of the market, eliminates unnecessary intermediaries that add costs, increase the carbon footprint of transportation, and reduce product freshness. Products sold at markets are usually locally or regionally produced, which significantly reduces transportation distances and associated emissions compared to supermarket products that often travel thousands of kilometers before reaching the shelf. The sale of seasonal products is another sustainable characteristic of markets: by offering only what is in season, markets promote diverse eating that respects natural cycles, in contrast to the artificial year-round availability promoted by supermarkets. Packaging at markets is usually minimal: products are sold by unit or by weight, and the buyer can bring their own bags and containers, drastically reducing packaging waste generation. Markets also contribute to economic sustainability by keeping small producers and traders alive who might otherwise be ruined by competition from large retailers. The preservation of local varieties of fruits, vegetables, and other products, which may not be profitable for industrial production but are valuable from a genetic and cultural perspective, is another benefit of traditional markets. ### 4.2 Planning electronics purchases

Before acquiring an electronic product, careful planning helps us maximize the quality-price ratio, avoid impulse purchases, and ensure that the acquired product truly adapts to our needs. The first step of planning is to clearly define what we need the product for, which functions are essential and which are simply desirable, establishing a priority-ordered requirements list. Establishing a realistic budget before going shopping is fundamental to keeping within our financial limits and avoiding the temptation of products that exceed our purchasing power. Researching the technical specifications of the products that interest us allows us to compare options objectively, based on concrete data like processor speed, storage capacity, screen quality, or battery life. Consulting reviews and opinions from other buyers on specialized portals like TechRadar, Wirecutter, or user forums provides us with a real perspective on the product's performance in daily use, its strengths, and its possible defects. Verifying the return policies and warranty of the store we plan to visit gives us peace of mind and flexibility in case the product doesn't work properly or doesn't convince us when trying it at home. Considering the compatibility of the new product with devices we already own is a crucial aspect that is often overlooked, since an incompatible product will represent a useless investment. Researching prices in different stores before deciding allows us to find the best deal and avoid unnecessary overpayment. Planning electronics purchases is a time investment that allows us to make more informed decisions, maximize the value of our investment, and fully enjoy the acquired product.

### 4.3 Technical aspects to consider

Technical aspects are the most determining factor in purchasing electronic products, as they determine the performance, durability, and long-term utility of the product. For smartphones, key technical aspects include processor type, RAM memory capacity, internal storage capacity, screen quality and size, camera capacity and quality, battery life, and compatibility with mobile phone networks. For laptops, technical aspects include processor type, RAM memory, hard drive or SSD capacity, screen size and resolution, graphics card, battery autonomy, and device weight. For appliances, technical aspects include energy efficiency, capacity, available functions, noise level, and ease of cleaning and maintenance. Build material quality is another important technical aspect that affects product durability, including the type of plastic or metal used in the casing, the quality of touch screens, and resistance to drops and scratches. Compatibility with connectivity standards and protocols, such as Bluetooth, WiFi, USB-C, or HDMI, is a technical aspect that determines the product's compatibility with other devices and accessories. Product performance in comparative tests and benchmarks published by independent testing laboratories provides objective data about its performance capabilities in different tasks. Technical aspects are the foundation for an informed purchasing decision in the electronics sector.

### 4.4 Additional services from electronics stores

Additional services are an important differentiating factor in electronics stores, as they provide added value to the acquired product and improve the customer experience. Post-sale technical service is one of the most valued services, as it guarantees that the product's technical problems will be resolved quickly and effectively by qualified technicians. Warranty extension is another important service that allows the buyer to extend the product's warranty coverage beyond the legal period, providing additional protection against failures and breakdowns. Installation and setup service is especially valuable for complex products like appliances, audio systems, or computer equipment, as it ensures that the product is installed correctly and works from day one. Customization service, which includes engraving, accessory installation, or personalized product configuration, allows the buyer to adapt the product to their specific needs. Loyalty programs and discounts for regular customers encourage repeat purchases and create a lasting relationship between the customer and the store. Financing and installment payment services allow the buyer to acquire high-cost products without needing to pay the full amount at the time of purchase. Home delivery and installation service is especially valuable for large, heavy products like appliances, televisions, or sound systems. Additional services are an important factor to consider when choosing an electronics store, as they can make a significant difference in the overall shopping experience and satisfaction with the acquired product.

## Chapter 5: In a Specialty Food Store

### 5.1 Gourmet and delicatessen stores

Gourmet and delicatessen stores are establishments specializing in the sale of high-quality foods, artisanal products, and exotic ingredients that are not easily found in conventional supermarkets. These stores offer a premium shopping experience, with personalized attention, expert advice, and a carefully curated selection of products from producers around the world. Gourmet stores usually specialize in a specific type of product, such as cheeses, cured meats, wines, chocolate, coffee, or olive oils, and offer an exceptional variety of options within their specialty. Gourmet store staff generally possess deep knowledge of the products they sell, which allows them to recommend the best option to customers based on their tastes and needs. Gourmet stores also usually offer additional services such as product tastings, cooking workshops, and personalized gift deliveries, which enrich the shopping experience and create a lasting relationship with customers. Product selection in gourmet stores is based on criteria of quality, flavor, and originality, prioritizing products with protected designation of origin, artisanal production, and traditional preparation processes. Gourmet stores are a space where gastronomy becomes a complete sensory experience, inviting customers to discover new flavors and textures that elevate the culinary experience.

### 5.2 Artisan bakeries and pastry shops

Artisan bakeries represent a centuries-old tradition that combines baking craftsmanship with the quality of natural ingredients to create products that delight the palate and nourish the body. Unlike industrial bakeries that use additives, preservatives, and processed flours, artisan bakeries prepare their products with slow-ground flours, natural yeast, sea salt, and water, following traditional kneading, fermentation, and baking techniques that develop complex flavors and unmatched textures. Sourdough bread, made with natural yeasts cultivated for days or even months, offers easier digestion, a crispy crust, and a spongy crumb with acidic notes that industrial breads cannot replicate. Artisan pastry shops create desserts and sweets that are true works of art, from Sacher tortes to cream eclairs, including macarons, croissants, and puff pastry baked goods made with first-quality butter. The relationship with customers in artisan bakeries is especially close, as many regular customers are known by name and their preferences, allowing for personalized attention that enriches the shopping experience. Artisan bakeries also function as social gathering spaces where neighbors meet to have a coffee and chat, strengthening the community fabric of the neighborhood. Choosing an artisan bakery requires identifying quality indicators like the aroma of freshly baked bread, the golden crispy crust, the irregular spongy crumb, and the absence of artificial additives in the ingredient list. Artisan bakeries are a living testament to the importance of preserving culinary traditions in an increasingly industrialized world, offering products that connect with our culinary roots and remind us of the pleasure of eating well.

### 5.3 Butcher shops and delis

Butcher shops and delis are establishments specializing in the sale of fresh and prepared meat products, offering a variety of cuts, sausages, and prepared items that reflect the culinary traditions of each region. A good butcher shop is distinguished by the freshness and quality of its products, the staff's knowledge of the different cuts and their culinary uses, and the personalized attention to customers. Fresh meat cuts are carefully displayed in the showcase, with uniform colors, firm textures, and absence of unpleasant odors that indicate lack of freshness. The butcher shop also offers a variety of artisanal sausages made with traditional recipes, such as Serrano ham, Iberian chorizo, salchichon, fuet, and loin, which are true delicatessen of the charcuterie. Butcher shop staff is generally expert in their craft, able to advise the customer on the best piece for each culinary preparation, cooking times, marinating techniques, and the most appropriate flavor combinations. Butcher shops also usually offer personalized preparation services, such as custom cutting, preparation of ground meat, preparation of skewers, and roasting of whole pieces. The relationship with the butcher is especially valuable for food lovers, as a trusted butcher can set aside the best pieces for regular customers, notify them of the arrival of special cuts, and offer preferential prices. Butcher shops and delis are temples of culinary tradition that preserve ancestral techniques for preparing meat products, offering the buyer a quality and variety that supermarkets can hardly match.

### 5.4 Fish markets

Fish markets are establishments specializing in the sale of fresh, frozen, and prepared fish and seafood, offering a variety of sea products ranging from whole fish to live seafood, including fillets, pickled fish, and artisanal preserves. A good fish market is distinguished by the freshness and quality of its products, which are identified by indicators like transparent, bulging eyes, bright, adhered scales, bright red gills, firm flesh that recovers its shape when pressed, and a clean sea smell, never ammonia. Fish market staff is generally expert in their craft, able to identify different species, recommend the best option for each culinary preparation, and fillet or clean fish according to the customer's needs. Fish markets also offer live seafood, such as shrimp, prawns, langoustines, and barnacles, which are considered the freshest and most flavorful products from the sea. The variety of products available at a fish market depends on the store's geographic location, the time of year, and the day's catch, ensuring a diverse offer adapted to market conditions. Fish markets also usually offer preparation services such as filleting, deboning, cleaning, and the preparation of fish products for different recipes. The relationship with the fishmonger is especially valuable for fish and seafood lovers, as a trusted fishmonger can set aside the best catch of the day, notify of the arrival of seasonal species, and offer culinary advice based on experience and knowledge. Fish markets are guardians of the maritime tradition that connect the consumer with the origin of sea products, offering a freshness and quality that are difficult to find in other distribution channels.

Artisanal and locally manufactured furniture stores offer unique pieces made by artisans using traditional techniques, offering customization and exclusivity that large retailers cannot provide. ### 6.2 Principles of interior decoration

Interior decoration is the art of creating livable spaces that are functional, aesthetic, and welcoming, combining elements like color, texture, lighting, furniture, and accessories to create environments that reflect the personality and needs of their inhabitants. The principles of interior decoration include harmony, balance, proportion, rhythm, and unity, which are the foundations for creating visually pleasing and functionally efficient spaces. Harmony is achieved when all elements of the space work together to create a sense of coherence and unity, avoiding discordant or overly eclectic combinations. Balance can be symmetrical, when elements are distributed equally on both sides of a central axis, or asymmetrical, when elements are distributed unequally but visually balanced. Proportion refers to the relationship between the size of elements and the size of the space, and scale refers to the relationship between the size of elements and the size of the humans who use them. Rhythm is created through the repetition of elements, such as colors, shapes, or textures, that guide the eye through the space and create a sense of movement. Unity is achieved when all elements of the space contribute to a coherent and consistent vision, creating a global atmosphere that is pleasant and functional. Interior decoration is a creative process that requires a balance between aesthetics and functionality, and that is nourished by experimentation, observation, and artistic sensitivity.

### 6.3 Choosing furniture

Choosing furniture is one of the most important decisions in interior decoration, as furniture is the element that defines the functionality and style of a space. Choosing furniture should consider factors like the size of the space, furniture layout, decorative style, comfort, durability, and available budget. The main furniture pieces in a home include the sofa, dining table, chairs, bed, wardrobes, and side tables, each of which serves a specific function and contributes to the comfort and organization of the space. Choosing the sofa, which is generally the most important piece in the living room, should consider factors like size, material, color, upholstery type, and the comfort of the seat and backrest. The dining table should be large enough to accommodate all diners and to place tableware and cutlery comfortably, and its style should be consistent with that of the chairs and the general decoration of the dining room. The bed is the most important piece in the bedroom, and its choice should prioritize mattress comfort, headboard quality, and storage functionality. Wardrobes and storage systems are fundamental for maintaining order and organization throughout the home, and should be chosen based on storage needs and available space. Choosing furniture is a long-term investment that deserves careful thought, as quality furniture lasts for decades and significantly contributes to the comfort and aesthetics of the home.

### 6.4 Lighting in decoration

Lighting is one of the most important and often underestimated elements in interior decoration, as it directly influences the perception of the space, the mood of its inhabitants, and the functionality of activities carried out in each area. Lighting can be classified into three main types: general lighting, which provides uniform light to the entire space; accent lighting, which highlights specific elements like artwork, plants, or architecture; and task lighting, which provides focused light for specific activities like reading, cooking, or working. The choice of light sources should consider factors like color temperature (warm, neutral, or cool), light intensity, light direction, and energy efficiency. Artificial light sources include incandescent, halogen, fluorescent, LED, and OLED bulbs, each with its own characteristics and advantages. Ceiling lamps provide general lighting, floor and table lamps provide accent and task lighting, and LED strips allow creating decorative light effects on furniture, shelves, and moldings. Natural lighting is a valuable resource that should be maximized, placing main furniture near windows and using decorative elements like mirrors to reflect natural light and expand the perception of space. Lighting is a powerful decorating tool that can completely transform a space, creating warm and welcoming, dramatic and elegant, or fresh and modern atmospheres simply by changing the amount, direction, and temperature of the light.

Urban gardening is a creative response to the lack of green spaces in cities, and represents an opportunity to reconnect people with nature and promote more sustainable and healthy lifestyles.

Fitness trackers and GPS watches are performance tracking tools that measure parameters like heart rate, calories burned, distance traveled, pace, and sleep quality, providing useful data to optimize our training. Sports water bottles are indispensable for staying hydrated during physical activity, and should be made of resistant materials, easy to clean, and with a design that allows comfortable drinking while exercising. Microfiber sports towels are lightweight, absorbent, and quick-drying, ideal for drying sweat during and after training. Resistance bands are versatile, portable training tools that allow performing strength, flexibility, and rehabilitation exercises anywhere. Proper care of sportswear is fundamental for maintaining its technical properties, extending its lifespan, and ensuring its performance during physical activity. The synthetic fabrics used in sportswear require specific care different from conventional clothing, as improper washing can damage fibers, reduce breathability, and eliminate sweat absorption properties. Sportswear washing should be done with cold or warm water (never hot), using mild detergent and avoiding fabric softeners, which can clog the fabric pores and reduce breathability. Sportswear should be washed inside out to protect seams and colors, and should be separated from conventional clothing to prevent fiber and odor transfer. Sportswear drying should be done outdoors, avoiding the dryer which can damage synthetic fibers and reduce fabric elasticity. Sportswear storage should be done in a dry, ventilated area, avoiding moisture that can cause the appearance of mold and unpleasant odors. Proper care of sportswear not only extends its lifespan but also ensures that its technical properties remain intact, providing us with maximum performance and comfort during each training session.

### 10.2 Pet food

Pet food is one of the most important aspects of animal care, as a proper diet is fundamental for ensuring their health, well-being, and longevity. Pet foods are classified into several categories, including dry kibble, wet food, fresh food, and BARF diets (Biologically Appropriate Raw Food), each with its own advantages and disadvantages. Dry kibble is the most common and practical food for dogs and cats, as it keeps for a long time, is easy to portion, and contributes to dental health due to its crunchy texture. Wet food, in cans or pouches, is more palatable for animals and contains more moisture, which is beneficial for kidney and urinary health, especially in cats. Fresh food, prepared with natural ingredients and without preservatives, offers a more varied and nutritious diet but requires refrigeration and has a shorter shelf life. BARF diets, based on raw meat, bones, and organs, aim to replicate the natural diet of dogs' and cats' ancestors but require careful handling to avoid health problems. The choice of appropriate food should consider factors like the animal's age, size, breed, activity level, and special needs, such as food allergies or specific health conditions. Detailed reading of pet food labels is fundamental for identifying quality ingredients, avoiding artificial additives, and ensuring the food provides all the necessary nutrients for the animal's health.

### 10.3 Pet accessories

Pet accessories are elements that improve the animal's quality of life and facilitate its care by the owner. The most common accessories include beds, carriers, feeders, water bowls, harnesses, leashes, toys, brushes, and interactive toys. Pet beds should be comfortable, washable, and appropriately sized for the animal, providing a safe and cozy place to rest. Carriers are indispensable for the safe transport of the animal by car, airplane, or public transportation, and should be appropriate for the animal's size and transport regulations. Feeders and water bowls should be made of safe materials, easy to clean, and appropriately sized for the animal, and can be automatic or manual depending on the owner's needs. Harnesses and leashes are safety elements that allow walking the animal in a controlled manner, and should be comfortable, durable, and adjusted to the animal's size. Toys are essential for the animal's entertainment and exercise, and should be appropriate for the animal's size, age, and temperament to prevent accidents. Brushes and grooming tools are important for keeping the animal's coat clean and healthy, and should be selected based on the animal's coat type. Pet accessories are investments in the animal's well-being that deserve a careful choice, prioritizing quality, safety, and functionality over price.

### 10.4 Pet health

Pet health is a fundamental responsibility of the owner, who must ensure that the animal receives proper veterinary attention, a balanced diet, and a safe and stimulating environment. Regular veterinary check-ups are essential for detecting health problems early and preventing diseases, and should be performed at least once a year for young animals and twice a year for senior animals. Vaccinations are mandatory for preventing serious infectious diseases like distemper, parvovirus, or rabies in dogs, and panleukopenia or feline leukemia in cats. Internal and external deworming is fundamental for protecting the animal from parasites like intestinal worms, fleas, and ticks, which can cause serious health problems. Sterilization or castration is an important decision that should be made in consultation with the veterinarian, as it has benefits for both the animal's health and the control of the abandoned animal population. Animal identification through microchip is mandatory in many countries and is fundamental for being able to recover the animal in case of loss or straying. Regular exercise and mental stimulation are important components of pet health, as they prevent obesity, reduce stress, and strengthen the bond between the animal and its owner. Pet health is a long-term commitment that requires attention, knowledge, and dedication from the owner.

### 12.4 Perfumes

Perfume notes are organized in three layers: top notes, which are the first we perceive and evaporate quickly; heart notes, which are the soul of the perfume and last for several hours; and base notes, which are the most intense and remain on the skin the longest. The most used ingredients in perfumery include flowers (rose, jasmine, lily), fruits (citrus, berries, tropical fruits), spices (vanilla, cinnamon, pepper), woods (sandalwood, cedar, vetiver), and ambers and musks, each with its own personality and character. Perfume application should be done on the wrists, behind the ears, on the neck, and on the wrists, where body temperature helps diffuse the fragrance and release its notes gradually. Perfumes are a form of personal expression that completes our image and leaves a lasting impression on those around us.
