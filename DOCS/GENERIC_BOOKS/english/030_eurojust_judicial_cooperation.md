# Eurojust and Judicial Cooperation in the EU

## Chapter 1: Foundations of Eurojust

### The Origins and Creation of Eurojust

Eurojust, the EU Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation, is the European agency responsible for facilitating judicial cooperation between Member States of the European Union in the fight against serious crime affecting two or more Member States. The origins of Eurojust date back to the Convention on Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters between the Member States of the European Union of 2000, which established the need to create a mechanism to facilitate judicial cooperation between Member States. Eurojust was created by the Council of the EU in 2002 as a judicial cooperation unit, and became an EU agency by the Council Regulation of 2002, which defined its mission, competencies, and institutional structure. Eurojust began its operations in 2003 and has grown significantly since then, becoming a fundamental agency for judicial cooperation in the European Union.

The creation of Eurojust was motivated by the need to strengthen judicial cooperation between Member States to address the growing international organized crime, which had benefited from the elimination of internal borders in the Schengen area and the free movement of persons, goods, and services in the internal market. Member States recognized that fighting international crime required closer and more effective judicial cooperation, and that Eurojust could provide the tools and mechanisms necessary to facilitate this cooperation. The mission of Eurojust is to support and reinforce the coordination and cooperation between the judicial authorities of Member States in the fight against serious crime affecting two or more Member States, including terrorism, illicit drug trafficking, human trafficking, cybercrime, fraud, money laundering, corruption, and economic and financial crime.

### Eurojust's Competencies

Eurojust has competencies in a wide range of serious crime areas affecting two or more Member States, including terrorism, illicit drug trafficking, human trafficking, cybercrime, fraud, money laundering, corruption, economic and financial crime, document forgery, arms trafficking, and sexual exploitation. Eurojust does not have the authority to investigate or prosecute crimes directly; rather, its role is to support the judicial authorities of Member States by providing judicial cooperation facilities, intelligence analysis, investigative support, and technical assistance in serious crime investigations. Eurojust also coordinates joint judicial investigations between Member States, facilitates the execution of mutual legal assistance requests, and provides logistical and technical support for serious crime investigations.

Eurojust's competencies extend to judicial cooperation with third countries and international organizations. Eurojust maintains judicial cooperation relations with the judicial authorities of numerous non-EU countries, including the United States, the United Kingdom, Norway, Switzerland, Canada, Australia, Colombia, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Peru, Mexico, Turkey, Israel, Japan, South Korea, China, India, Pakistan, Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, Georgia, Moldova, Ukraine, and other countries, through cooperation agreements that facilitate judicial cooperation and information exchange in matters of serious crime. Eurojust also cooperates with international organizations such as Interpol, the United Nations, and the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE), to promote international judicial cooperation and to coordinate actions against serious crime.

## Chapter 2: The Institutional Structure of Eurojust

### The President and the Presidency of Eurojust

The President of Eurojust is the highest authority of the agency and has the responsibility to direct and manage the activities of Eurojust, to represent Eurojust before the judicial authorities of Member States, EU institutions, and international organizations, and to implement the decisions of the Eurojust College. The President is elected by the Eurojust College, which is composed of one representative from each Member State, for a four-year term renewable once. The President has the responsibility to ensure compliance with the mission and competencies of Eurojust, to manage the agency's human and financial resources, to maintain relations with the judicial authorities of Member States, EU institutions, and international organizations, and to ensure the quality and relevance of the services provided by Eurojust to the judicial authorities of Member States.

The presidency of Eurojust consists of the President and the Vice-Presidents, who are appointed by the Eurojust College on the proposal of the President. The Vice-Presidents have the responsibility to lead the units and departments of Eurojust, to coordinate the operational and analytical activities of the agency, and to support the President in the management and direction of Eurojust. The organizational structure of Eurojust includes the Judicial Cooperation Division, which coordinates Eurojust's judicial cooperation activities; the Criminal Analysis Division, which provides intelligence analysis and analytical support to the judicial authorities of Member States; the National Desks Division, which manages Eurojust's relations with the judicial authorities of Member States; and the Administrative and Financial Division, which manages the agency's human and financial resources. Eurojust currently has more than 250 employees, including judges, prosecutors, analysts, and administrative staff, and its annual budget exceeds 70 million euros.

### The Eurojust College and the European Parliament

The Eurojust College is the decision-making body of the agency and is composed of one representative from each Member State, who is usually a judge or prosecutor designated by the national judicial authorities. The Eurojust College has the responsibility to adopt decisions on the strategic and operational priorities of Eurojust, to approve the annual budget and the work plan of the agency, to appoint and dismiss the President and the Vice-Presidents, and to oversee the activities of Eurojust. The Eurojust College meets at least four times a year to discuss the priorities and activities of Eurojust, and may hold extraordinary meetings when circumstances require. The Eurojust College also establishes working groups and committees that advise the executive management of Eurojust on strategic priorities, international cooperation, and budget matters.

The European Parliament plays an important role in overseeing the activities of Eurojust, under the provisions of the Treaty of Lisbon that establish the European Parliament's accountability towards EU agencies. The European Parliament receives regular information on Eurojust's activities through the European Parliament's Committee on Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs, which is the committee responsible for overseeing Eurojust's activities. The European Parliament also approves Eurojust's annual budget and supervises the agency's financial management. The President of Eurojust appears before the European Parliament's Committee on Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs to answer questions from Members of the European Parliament about Eurojust's activities, and the European Parliament may request additional information on Eurojust's activities at any time. The European Parliament's oversight ensures the accountability of Eurojust and the transparency of its activities.

## Chapter 3: Judicial Cooperation Tools

### European Arrest Warrants and Mutual Legal Assistance Requests

European arrest warrants are a fundamental instrument of judicial cooperation in the European Union, which allows the judicial authorities of one Member State to request the arrest and surrender of a person to another Member State for prosecution or the execution of a sentence. European arrest warrants were established by the Framework Decision of 2002 on the European arrest warrant and the surrender procedures between Member States, which replaced the previous extradition procedures based on international conventions. European arrest warrants have simplified and accelerated the surrender procedures between Member States, as the execution deadlines are shorter, the grounds for refusal are more limited, and communication between judicial authorities is more direct. Eurojust plays an important role in the execution of European arrest warrants, providing technical assistance and facilitating communication between the judicial authorities of Member States.

Mutual legal assistance requests are another fundamental instrument of judicial cooperation in the European Union, which allows the judicial authorities of one Member State to request another Member State to carry out judicial acts in its territory, including obtaining evidence, executing search warrants, intercepting communications, and arresting suspects. Mutual legal assistance requests are governed by the Convention on Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters between the Member States of the European Union of 2000, which establishes the procedures and conditions for the execution of mutual legal assistance requests. Eurojust plays an important role in the execution of mutual legal assistance requests, providing technical assistance and facilitating communication between the judicial authorities of Member States. Eurojust has also established the European Judicial Network, which facilitates informal cooperation between the judicial authorities of Member States for the execution of mutual legal assistance requests.

### Joint Judicial Investigations

Joint judicial investigations are a fundamental instrument of judicial cooperation in the European Union, which allows the judicial authorities of several Member States to coordinate their investigations in cases of serious crime affecting multiple countries. Joint judicial investigations are governed by the Convention on Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters of 2002, which establishes the legal framework for the creation of joint investigation teams between Member States. Joint investigation teams are working groups composed of judges, prosecutors, and other judicial officials from several Member States, who coordinate their investigations in cases of serious crime that require the collection of evidence in several countries. Eurojust plays an important role in the creation and coordination of joint investigation teams, providing technical assistance, facilitating communication between Member States, and coordinating the actions of the teams.

Joint judicial investigations are particularly useful for combating international organized crime, which often requires the collection of evidence in several countries and the coordination of judicial actions between Member States. Joint investigation teams can coordinate judicial acts in several countries, including obtaining evidence, executing search warrants, intercepting communications, and arresting suspects. Eurojust has supported the creation of numerous joint investigation teams in recent years, in cases of serious crime such as terrorism, illicit drug trafficking, human trafficking, cybercrime, fraud, money laundering, and corruption. Joint judicial investigations have proven to be an effective instrument for combating international organized crime, as they allow the coordination of judicial actions between Member States and the collection of evidence in several countries in a coordinated and efficient manner.

The Convention on Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters between the Member States of the European Union of 2000 is the fundamental normative instrument governing mutual legal assistance in the European Union, and establishes the conditions and procedures for the judicial authorities of one Member State to request judicial assistance from the judicial authorities of another Member State in the investigation and prosecution of serious crime. The Convention establishes a wide range of mutual legal assistance measures, including the taking of statements, the execution of judicial orders, the obtaining of evidence, the identification and tracing of persons, the surveillance of communications, and the transfer of detained persons to give testimony. The Convention also establishes deadlines for the execution of mutual legal assistance requests, and provides for the possibility of requesting urgent assistance in emergency cases.
