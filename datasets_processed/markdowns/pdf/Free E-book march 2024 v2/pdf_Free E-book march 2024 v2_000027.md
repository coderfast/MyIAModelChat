<|en|><|problem|>And after a few minutes, we have the result of the low quality test render that we can play back. I recommend always rendering a low quality quick render before you commit to rendering high quality frames. If the result is not to your liking, adjust the keyframes to make it more interesting. Once you’ve got something that you’re happy with, you can set the resolution back to 4K for the final render. And OpenEXR for the file format. We’ll render an image sequence. 16 bit, or half float, is enough to be able to do some nice color grading. The advantage of rendering an image sequence instead of a video file, is that when the render stops at some point (for example, because of a computer or software crash), you won’t lose the frames rendered up to that point. And you can just continue rendering from where it left off.<|final|>
